# 1. PURPOSE OF THE EVIDENCE LEDGER

The Evidence Ledger is the central collection of historical questions that deserve investigation.

It should be intentionally broad.

We are not restricting the investigation to discoveries already accepted by mainstream historical institutions. We are also not treating alternative-history claims as established facts.

The objective is to collect everything potentially relevant, investigate its origins, evaluate its evidence, and connect it to the larger reconstruction of human history.

The ledger should include:

- Archaeological discoveries that challenge established chronologies.
- Unusual prehistoric structures.
- Unexplained changes in human evolution.
- Ancient genetic and genealogical mysteries.
- Ancient religious and historical accounts.
- Lost civilizations.
- Ancient maps and cartographic anomalies.
- Unexplained artifacts.
- Ancient astronomical knowledge.
- Unusual similarities between geographically separated cultures.
- Historical accounts of aerial phenomena.
- Modern UAP and UFO investigations.
- Military and government disclosures.
- Esoteric historical movements.
- Geological and environmental anomalies.
- Alternative interpretations of established discoveries.

**No entry should be excluded simply because it is controversial. No entry should be promoted to historical fact simply because it is interesting.**

# 2. EVIDENCE CLASSIFICATION SYSTEM

Every entry must have two separate classifications:

1. The reliability of the underlying evidence.
2. The reliability of the proposed interpretation.

These are not necessarily the same.

For example, Göbekli Tepe is an established archaeological site. The interpretation that it demonstrates a lost technologically advanced civilization is a separate and much less established claim.

## Evidence status

| Classification | Description |
|---|---|
| E1 — Established | Independently documented and supported by strong evidence. |
| E2 — Strong Evidence | Well-supported discovery with meaningful unresolved questions. |
| E3 — Credible Dispute | Genuine disagreement about chronology, interpretation, or significance. |
| E4 — Unresolved Anomaly | Real evidence exists, but no satisfactory explanation is established. |
| E5 — Contested Claim | A claim exists, but its evidence or interpretation is disputed. |
| E6 — Speculative Hypothesis | An interesting possibility requiring substantial additional evidence. |
| E7 — Unsupported Claim | Insufficient reliable evidence currently supports the claim. |
| E8 — Contradicted | Strong evidence conflicts with the proposed explanation. |

# 3. PREHISTORIC CIVILIZATION AND CHRONOLOGICAL ANOMALIES

These discoveries challenge simplistic assumptions about when complex human societies emerged.

## 3.1 Göbekli Tepe — Monumental Architecture Before Conventional Agriculture

**Location:** Southeastern Turkey  
**Approximate age:** Beginning around 9600 BCE.

**Discovery:**
- Large monumental stone enclosures.
- Massive T-shaped limestone pillars.
- Elaborate animal carvings.
- Organized architectural construction.
- Evidence of complex symbolic and ritual activity.

**Why it matters:**

Göbekli Tepe demonstrates that monumental architecture and substantial collective construction existed among hunter-gatherer communities much earlier than many older models of civilization development anticipated.

It raises questions about:
- Social organization before agriculture.
- Labor coordination.
- Specialized craftsmanship.
- Religious institutions.
- Food production and settlement patterns.
- The relationship between ritual centers and agricultural development.

**Questions to investigate:**
- How did large construction projects get organized?
- What was the relationship between Göbekli Tepe and nearby settlements?
- How much of the surrounding archaeological landscape remains undiscovered?
- How did the site's construction phases develop?
- What other monumental centers existed in the region?

**Evidence status:** E1 — Established site; broader interpretations remain open.

2. Symbolic account of shared human origins.
3. Ancient metaphor describing reproduction and biological kinship.
4. Cultural memory of a population bottleneck or founding population.

## 12.3 Nuclear Weapons and UAP Reports

**Major research priority.**

Investigate reports of unusual aerial phenomena associated with nuclear weapons facilities, nuclear testing, and strategic military installations.

# 22. FINAL PRINCIPLE

Human history should be investigated as an unfinished scientific and historical reconstruction.

The archaeological record is incomplete. Ancient texts are imperfect records of human memory. Genetics reveals population histories that written history cannot. Geological evidence reveals environmental events that may have transformed entire regions. Modern technology provides new ways to investigate old questions.

We should not assume that the conventional timeline contains every important chapter.

We should not assume that every alternative theory is correct.

We should not allow the limitations of current knowledge to become the limits of our curiosity.

**The ledger exists to preserve the questions, collect the evidence, test the explanations, and continuously improve our reconstruction of the human past.**
