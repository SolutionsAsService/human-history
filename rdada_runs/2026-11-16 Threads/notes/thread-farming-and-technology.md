# thread-farming-and-technology: Farming and technology across Eurasia and Oceania

## Ask
What do ancient DNA and archaeology show about the spread of farming from the Fertile Crescent into Europe and beyond (migration versus cultural diffusion), and the spread of bronze and iron working, the horse and wheeled vehicles, and Austronesian seafaring? Which parts are settled and which disputed?

## Cited findings
- Aegean and Anatolian Neolithic genomes (5 individuals) are close to early farmers across Europe, extending the "migratory chain" to southwestern Asia; the authors also state that migration, diffusion and forager admixture remain open questions — [Hofmanová et al. 2016, White Rose record](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/117992/)
- Vanuatu ancient genomes: first Remote Oceanians of East Asian (Austronesian-linked) ancestry, then a turnover with Papuan ancestry between about 3000 and 2400 BP — [Lipson et al., ORA manuscript](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:b198cb4d-4656-417a-aa5a-a1230060bc5a/files/m535a9bf106a6e317b8cbc769347dc06c)
- Cattle: zebu introgression into Near Eastern cattle about 4.2 ka (animal transfer, carrier unknown) — [Verdugo et al. 2019](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/149232/)
- Cultural diffusion without much migration is documented for dairy pastoralism on the eastern steppe by 1300 BCE — [Jeong et al. 2018](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/149598/)
- Domestic (DOM2) horses trace to the lower Don-Volga about 2200 BCE; Botai is separate — [ORA supplement](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:9de5041d-93bc-49df-8a99-5668e628bc9e/files/rbc386k59w)
- Iron: limited Anatolian origin early in the 2nd millennium BC, wide spread only late 2nd to early 1st millennium BC — [Erb-Satullo 2019, ORA](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:8b406295-a086-48b1-aa7a-f66c4c035b07)

## Sceptical and alternative pass
- Farming: the Hofmanová abstract itself states the uncertainty; Wikipedia's Neolithic Europe page (pointer only) records that pre-aDNA demic-diffusion clines were called speculative. The dairy paper shows that the eastern steppe was a diffusion case, so "migration" is not a rule.
- Horse: Levine, Bailey et al. (2000) say redating undid the Dereivka claim and that bit wear is ambiguous. Search results mention a reassessment of Botai (PMC) but I did not open it.
- Tin bronze: a Colchis crucible raises the possibility of local tin (a single fragment).
- Austronesian: Wikipedia (pointer only) lists the Out-of-Sundaland rival; the Vanuatu turnover complicates simple continuity.
- Settled vs disputed (from the opened sources): Anatolia-to-Europe farming ancestry is well supported by aDNA, while the proportions of forager admixture vary by region and are not settled in these sources. Horse domestication origin is dated by aDNA to about 2200 BCE for DOM2; earlier claims (Dereivka, Botai) are disputed. The origin and route of the wheel is unresolved in the sources I opened (Wikipedia pointer only). Bronze and iron: iron's Anatolian origin is "supported" with limits; copper and tin routes are only partly covered (see Gaps).
- Support is stronger than the sceptical side for farming and horses; the evidence for metallurgy and the wheel is thinner.
