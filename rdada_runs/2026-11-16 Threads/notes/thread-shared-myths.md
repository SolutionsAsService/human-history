# thread-shared-myths: Shared mythic motifs passed between distant cultures

## Ask
- Is there credible evidence for very old shared mythic motifs passed down between distant cultures (e.g. Witzel's Laurasian mythology, flood and world-parent myths, the Cosmic Hunt; Berezkin's folklore distribution)? What methods are used and what are the criticisms?
- What does the comparative-genetic study of oral traditions (e.g. phylogenetic studies of folktales) show, and how robust is it?

## Cited findings
- Witzel states the Laurasian thesis: a common story line "can be found in most of the mythologies of Eurasia, North Africa, Polynesia, and the Americas", traced back to the later Palaeolithic (c. 40,000 years ago) — [Witzel 2012, Origins of the World's Mythologies](https://archive.org/details/the-origins-of-the-worlds-mythologies)
- Witzel describes Berezkin's motif maps and principal-component analysis, and concedes they show a trend while "the actual geographical origin of a trait remains unclear" — same source
- Witzel argues the flood myth is old and not a late diffusion, while granting that similar flood myths may arise independently in river and coastal societies — same source
- Witzel rejects diffusion, Jungian archetypes and Levi-Straussian structures as sufficient explanations — same source
- Witzel dates the sun-moon temporary-marriage myth before the first entry into the Americas — [Witzel 2008, Slaying the dragon in Eurasia](https://archive.org/details/MichaelWitzelSlayingTheDragonInEurasia)
- d'Huy builds trees of 19 Cosmic Hunt versions (CI 0.59, RI 0.71) and claims low borrowing and Palaeolithic roots; he also admits modified-data runs "could be less reliable" and that clusters (Tuareg with Greek) may reflect borrowing — [d'Huy 2013](https://archive.org/details/a-cosmic-hunt-in-the-berber-sky-a-phylogenetic-d-huy-j)
