# thread-indo-european: Indo-European spread (steppe, Anatolian, Indo-Iranian)

## Ask
- What does ancient DNA, archaeology and historical linguistics show about the spread of Indo-European languages (steppe/Yamnaya hypothesis, Anatolian hypothesis, Indo-Iranian branch)? Dates, routes, and the strength of each hypothesis.
- What are the principal sceptical or alternative views, and what remains unresolved?

## Cited findings
- aDNA shows extensive gene flow into temperate Europe shortly after 3000 BC; Corded Ware is read as hybridisation of migrant Yamnaya people with local Neolithic groups, and the authors say migration can now be "documented rather than debated" (the people movement is documented; the language shift is inferred; authors are proponents) — [Kristiansen et al. 2017, Antiquity, OA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/retheorising-mobility-and-the-formation-of-culture-and-language-among-the-corded-ware-culture-in-europe/E35E6057F48118AFAC191BDFBB1EB30E)
- A linguist's summary: after the aDNA revolution Indo-European reached Europe and Central/South Asia from the late 4th millennium BCE with Yamnaya groups (preview only) — [Kloekhorst 2023, ch. 4](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/indoeuropean-puzzle-revisited/protoindoanatolian-the-anatolian-split-and-the-anatolian-trek-a-comparative-linguistic-perspective/F33EABD6C9A5CECD109293B8F238271C)
- Indo-Iranian branch: Sintashta is said to descend from Corded Ware-related peoples and to lead to Andronovo and Indo-Iranian (Wikipedia pointer only; Nature/Science papers not opened) — [Sintashta culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sintashta_culture)
- Eastward Indo-European speakers in Afanasievo and Andronovo are argued to be the source of six Bronze Age loanwords in Uralic, Turkic and Old Chinese — [Bjørn 2022, OA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/evolutionary-human-sciences/article/indoeuropean-loanwords-and-exchange-in-bronze-age-central-and-east-asia/91132871EAF737CD98BDFC43C16C761A)
- Anatolian hypothesis (Renfrew; Neolithic farming from about 7000 BC) is still described as the main competitor of the steppe theory, which "enjoys more academic favor" (Wikipedia pointer) — [Anatolian hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolian_hypothesis)
- Heggarty (2014, before the 2015 aDNA papers) reads Bayesian phylogenetics as favouring an Anatolian origin — [Antiquity abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/prehistory-by-bayesian-phylogenetics-the-state-of-the-art-on-indoeuropean-origins/B540C4E33296C2E8CA758EE005E80FB9)
- A 2024 review says the Anatolian branch is the earliest split and that the route of Anatolian entry (west vs other) is not settled — [Bjørn 2024, OA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/twentyfirstcentury-light-over-the-indoeuropean-homeland-triangulating-language-archaeology-and-genetics/514BDF555526848F87F108F8F04544E6)

## Sceptical and alternative pass
- Klejn vs the Nature authors (EJA 2017): published exchange, Klejn unconvinced; abstract only, arguments unread.
- Furholt (EJA 2017): accepts the aDNA data, attacks the culture-historical "massive migration" narrative; says it is not discussing the Indo-European question itself.
- "Beyond binaries" (Archaeological Dialogues): general theoretical critique of aDNA archaeology; abstract only.
- Anatolian/Bayesian side: Heggarty 2014; Garrett and Anthony objections to Bouckaert 2012 reported via Wikipedia (original not opened).
- Not found/opened: Kassian and Starostin 2025 critique of the Heggarty 2023 hybrid model; Heggarty 2023 itself; Lazaridis 2025.
- One-sidedness: open coverage of the steppe case rests on proponents' own papers (Kristiansen et al., Kloekhorst); the primary aDNA papers (Haak 2015, Allentoft 2015, Damgaard 2018, Narasimhan 2019) and the primary Bayesian papers (Bouckaert 2012, Heggarty 2023) are not among the candidates. Dates and routes for Indo-Iranian into Iran/India are therefore unsupported here beyond a Wikipedia pointer.
