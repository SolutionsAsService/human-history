# thread-writing-systems: Lineage of writing systems

## Ask
- What is the documented lineage of writing systems: Egyptian hieroglyphs and Proto-Sinaitic to Phoenician to Greek to Latin and Cyrillic; cuneiform to Old Persian; Aramaic to Brahmi, Arabic and Hebrew scripts? Dates, inscriptions, and what is debated (e.g. Aramaic origin of Brahmi, independent invention of Chinese and Mesoamerican scripts).
- Which links are documented by dated inscriptions and which are inferred from letter shapes?

## Cited findings
- Serabit el-Khadim (Sinai): Petrie found non-Egyptian signs on a sphinx and about eight tablets, argued it is "a definite system", not imitation of hieroglyphs, and dated it about 1500 BC; he saw it as one of several early alphabets, not the sole parent of Phoenician. — [Petrie 1906, Researches in Sinai](https://archive.org/details/researchesinsin00currgoog)
- Phoenician to Greek: Greek tradition (Herodotus 5.58) says Phoenicians brought the letters. — [Herodotus, Perseus](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0126:book=5:chapter=58)
- Modern grounds for that link are shapes, letter order and Semitic letter names (inference, not a dated handover); where, when and by whom are disputed (Cyprus thesis). — [Woodard 1997](https://archive.org/details/GreekWritingFromKnossosToHomerALinguisticInterpretationOfTheOriginOfTheGreekAlph)
- Date of adoption disputed across seven centuries; Semitic letter forms give a terminus post quem in the ninth century BC; the Dipylon oinochoe (c. 740 BC) is the early Attic text. — [Jeffery and Johnston 1990](https://archive.org/details/scriptarchaic)
- Greek to Etruscan to Latin via Chalcidian colonies at Cumae (inferred from letter forms). — [Taylor 1883](https://archive.org/details/alphabetaccounto01tayluoft); [Jeffery and Johnston 1990](https://archive.org/details/scriptarchaic)
- Greek to Russian (Cyrillic): "borrowed in the 9th century", per an 1883 passing remark only. — [Taylor 1883](https://archive.org/details/alphabetaccounto01tayluoft)
- Old Persian cuneiform: first attested under Darius I at Behistun (c. 520 BC); the Susian Epigraph L says he made inscriptions "in another fashion" and dispatched copies. Derivation from Mesopotamian cuneiform is inferred from sign structure. — [King and Thompson 1907](https://archive.org/details/sculpturesinscri00brituoft)
- Aramaic to Kharosthi under Darius's administration (Buhler's inference); Brahmi from a North Semitic source around 800 BC (Buhler, debated). — [Buhler 1898](https://archive.org/details/onoriginofindian00buhliala)
- Mesoamerican scripts treated as one regional descent from an earliest system, with nearly a dozen systems, most undeciphered. — [Clemmensen and Helmke 2023](https://archive.org/details/oapen-20.500.12657-77203)

## Sceptical and alternative pass
- Brahmi: Cunningham (1879) denies any Semitic borrowing (indigenous invention); Buhler lists five rival derivations including Halevy's composite Aramaic/Kharosthi/Greek theory. Both are 19th-century. The modern disputants (Salomon on the Aramaic case, Falk 2018 on creation under Ashoka, the 2023 hybrid-origin paper) were found by search but could not be opened.
- Proto-Sinaitic: Petrie's own caution (one of many alphabets) is the sceptical source; the dating is outdated by later scholarship I could not open.
- Phoenician to Greek: Woodard's Cypriot-syllabary thesis is the alternative on place and agency; Jeffery shows the date dispute.
- Chinese and Mesoamerican independence: only the Mesoamerican side was opened, and it assumes rather than tests independence. Nothing opened on Chinese.
- Almost all the opened sources are older secondary or primary works on archive.org; this pass is weighted to the 1880-1907 and 1990-1997 literature and is one-sided toward consensus on the Greek and Indian chains.
