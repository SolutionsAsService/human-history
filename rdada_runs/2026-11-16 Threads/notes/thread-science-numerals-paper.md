# thread-science-numerals-paper: Transmission of science and technology (numerals, astronomy, translation, paper, printing)

## Ask
- What is documented about the transmission of knowledge: Indian numerals and zero to the Islamic world to Europe (Fibonacci, al-Khwarizmi); Babylonian astronomy to Greece (Hipparchus); the Greek-to-Arabic-to-Latin translation movement; papermaking from China to the Islamic world to Europe; printing? Give dates, routes and the evidence.
- Which claims are disputed (date of the zero, 'Arabic numerals' naming, paper arrival, independent invention)?

## Cited findings
- Fibonacci's Liber Abbaci (1202) names "novem figure indorum", the nine figures of the Indians; Smith and Karpinski cite Boncompagni's 1857 edition. — [Smith & Karpinski, The Hindu-Arabic numerals (1911)](https://archive.org/stream/hinduarabicnumer00smitrich/hinduarabicnumer00smitrich_djvu.txt)
- Arab writers from the early ninth century recognised the Hindu origin of the numerals; the Arabic-to-Latin step runs through "Dixit algoritmi" (the Latin arithmetic) and Robert of Chester's translation of al-Khwarizmi's algebra at Segovia in 1145. — [Smith & Karpinski 1911](https://archive.org/stream/hinduarabicnumer00smitrich/hinduarabicnumer00smitrich_djvu.txt); [Karpinski 1915](https://archive.org/stream/robertofchesters00muha/robertofchesters00muha_djvu.txt)
- Al-Biruni (c. 1030) says Indian scholars brought the Brahmasiddhanta and Khandakhadyaka to al-Mansur's Baghdad (753-774) where al-Fazari translated them, and that the numeral signs in use derive from the best Hindu forms. — [Alberuni's India](https://archive.org/stream/alberunisindiaac01biru/alberunisindiaac01biru_djvu.txt)
- Greek-to-Arabic: almost all secular non-literary Greek books were translated c. 750-1000 under Abbasid patronage (Gutas). — [Gutas 1998](https://archive.org/stream/DimitriGutasGreekThoughtArabicCultureTheGraecoArabicTranslationMovementInBaghdad/Dimitri%20Gutas-Greek%20Thought,%20Arabic%20Culture_%20The%20Graeco-Arabic%20Translation%20Movement%20in%20Baghdad%20and%20Early%20'Abbasaid%20Society%20(2nd-4th%205th-10th%20c.)%20(Arabic%20Thought%20&%20Culture)%20%20-Routledge%20(1998)_djvu.txt)
- Zero: the 1911 consensus put the Gwalior inscription (876) as earliest sure Indian zero; the Oxford radiocarbon report now says the first 2017 measurement of folio 16 was inaccurate and models Bakhshali production at 799-1102 calCE. — [Chivall et al., ORA](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:5a6d1dd7-f20c-4209-adb6-33849f5b08f4/files/snp193c210)
- Hipparchus and Babylon, paper (Talas, Europe 1085-1150) and printing (Castaldi/Marco Polo) come only through Wikipedia pointers: [Hipparchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hipparchus), [History of paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_paper), [Movable type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movable_type).

## Sceptical and alternative pass
- "Arabic numerals" naming: Smith and Karpinski show Europeans of the 1500s called the numerals Arabic (and 'Barbaric') while Fibonacci and the Arabic writers called them Indian; so the label records the Latin route, not the origin. Only partly captured in a candidate (the Arab-recognition one).
- Zero date: the Oxford report (primary) undermines the 3rd-4th century dating of folio 16; the five-folio model puts the manuscript at 799-1102 calCE. The scholarly rebuttal of the 2017 press release (Wujastyk, Hayashi, Keller, Montelle, Plofker) was found but not opened.
- Direct vs mediated: Gutas has Indian science reaching Arabic mainly through Pahlavi (Persian) intermediaries, against al-Biruni's direct Indian pandits. Both are in candidates; they are not reconciled.
- Hipparchus: Wikipedia reports that his one surviving text cannot decide whether his methods were Babylonian, and that the Babylonian/Greek direction for the tropical year is debatable. Kugler's demonstration is the supporting side. One-sided in depth: no primary Hipparchus or cuneiform text was opened.
- Paper: Talas story is sceptically treated (Bloom via Wikipedia); the Central-Asia route and the Baghdad (794-795) and Europe (1085-1150) dates rest on Wikipedia alone here.
- Printing: Yule's scepticism about a Marco Polo transfer, via Wikipedia. Independent invention is not argued in any opened source.
- This run is thinner on paper, printing and Hipparchus than on numerals; the numeral strands have primary-era texts, the others pointers only.
