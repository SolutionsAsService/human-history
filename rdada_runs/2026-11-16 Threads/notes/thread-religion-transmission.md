# thread-religion-transmission: Transmission of religions and religious motifs between cultures

## Ask
What is documented about religions and religious motifs passing between cultures: Buddhism from India across the Silk Road to China, Korea and Japan; Mesopotamian flood and creation narratives into the Hebrew Bible; Egyptian Isis and Hellenistic mystery cults into Rome; Manichaeism? Give dates, routes and the evidence. What do sceptical or minimalist scholars say, and which resemblances are judged independent or coincidental?

## Cited findings
- Fa-hien (c. 399-414 CE) was told in the Indus region that Indian monks had earlier crossed the river carrying sutras and discipline books, and monks asked him when the Law first went east; Kushan (Yueh-she) kings appear as Buddhists. Oral tradition, not a dated text. — [Legge, Fa-hien's Record (1886)](https://archive.org/details/recordofbuddhist00fahsuoft)
- Chinese foundation story: Emperor Ming's dream and mission to India, AD 60 in Beal's rendering of the Han annals; Kasyapa Matanga as first Indian monk. — [Beal (1882)](https://archive.org/details/abstractoffourle00bealrich)
- Wikipedia pointer: 1st-century embassy is semi-legendary; extensive contacts from the 2nd century via Kushan expansion; overland versus maritime debate. — [Silk Road transmission of Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Road_transmission_of_Buddhism)
- Flood: George Smith's 1876 translation of the Deluge tablet (dove, swallow, raven) and his dating of Creation/Flood traditions to c. 2000-1850 BC; he also records divergences (Nizir versus Ararat). — [Smith (1876)](https://archive.org/details/chaldeanaccount00smitrich)
- Wikipedia pointer: three Mesopotamian flood accounts; many scholars think the Gilgamesh flood was taken from Atra-Hasis. — [Gilgamesh flood myth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilgamesh_flood_myth)
- Isis: Ptolemaic export to Cyprus, Sicily, Athens c. 307-298 BC; expulsions under Augustus and Tiberius; adoption under Caligula (Cumont, 1911, secondary). — [Cumont](https://archive.org/details/theorientalrelig00cumouoft)
- Isis in a Latin text of the 2nd century CE: Apuleius, Metamorphoses XI. — [Apuleius, Loeb](https://archive.org/details/goldenassbeingme00apuliala)
- Axial Age: Jaspers's thesis of independent parallel breakthroughs, disputed; Black (2008) calls Jaspers's description "highly defective" (abstract only). — [Black](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0034670508000168), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axial_Age)

## Sceptical and alternative pass
- Buddhism: the legendary status of the Ming-ti dream is flagged via the Wikipedia pointer only; no opened scholarly sceptic (Zurcher's borrow-only text did not open).
- Flood/creation: only Smith's own noted divergences and a Wikipedia pointer; a true minimalist source (Heidel 1949, Lambert, Millard) was not opened (Heidel on Internet Archive returned no text; thetorah.com, tyndalebulletin.org, core.ac.uk blocked).
- Isis: nothing sceptical opened; Cumont's framework is old and itself contested, not shown from an opened source.
- Axial Age is the only genuine independent-development/sceptic strand (Black abstract). The set is therefore one-sided on Buddhism, the Bible and Isis.
