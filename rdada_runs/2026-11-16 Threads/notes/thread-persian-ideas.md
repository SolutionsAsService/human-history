# thread-persian-ideas: Persian ideas, institutions and words passed on

## Ask
What ideas, institutions and words passed from Achaemenid and Sasanian Persia to neighbouring and later cultures (Second Temple Judaism, Greece and Hellenism, early Christianity, the Islamic Abbasid court, India, Central Asia, Ottoman/Urdu/Swahili loanwords)? State the dated evidence for each strand. What do sceptical specialists say about how far Zoroastrianism shaped Judaism and Christianity (angels, dualism, resurrection), and about Persian administrative models?

## Cited findings
- Satrapies named as a Persian institution, with the Persian term, by a 5th c. BCE Greek author — [Herodotus 3.89](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0126%3Abook%3D3%3Achapter%3D89)
- Relay-post (angareion) and its Persian name — [Herodotus 8.98](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0126%3Abook%3D8%3Achapter%3D98)
- Greeks knew Magian dualism and a future life by the 4th c. BCE (Theopompus, via Diogenes Laertius) — [Diogenes Laertius, Prologue](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0258)
- Cyrus and the Temple rebuilding, as told c. 1st c. CE — [Josephus, Ant. 11.1](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0146%3Abook%3D11%3Awhiston%20chapter%3D1)
- Loanword pardes appears in Hebrew only after 538 BCE; Greek paradeisos in Xenophon (Greek text opened at Perseus 1999.01.0201, Anabasis 1.2.7) — [Paradise (Wikipedia pointer)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise)
- Seleukid financial administration owed much to Achaemenid prototypes — [Aperghis thesis, UCL](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10115558/1/10115558_Aperghis_thesis.pdf)
- Late Sasanian involvement in Oman is polarised between historians and archaeologists — [Munt 2017, White Rose](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/124493/1/Oman_and_late_Sasanian_imperialism.pdf)
- Pahlavi texts in the Abbasid translation movement; Irano-Islamic secretarial class; Persian as Ottoman/Mughal bureaucratic language — Wikipedia pointers only (Graeco-Arabic translation movement, Barmakids, Persian language).
- Strong-influence position (Boyce) on Judaism, Christianity and Islam — Wikipedia pointer, secondhand.

## Sceptical and alternative pass
- Judaism/Christianity: the only sceptical material I could open is the Wikipedia Zoroastrianism article, which lists scholars who "diminish or reject" influence, and Grabbe's and Tso's framing (not whether, but where and how much; Iranian versus Greek). Searches also surfaced Barr's JAAR 1985 article (parallel lists versus structural similarity) and a Jewish Bible Quarterly piece stressing that detailed Zoroastrian sources are Sasanian-era; neither could be opened (see gaps), so neither is a candidate.
- Cyrus: the Wikipedia Cyrus Cylinder article records that the Jewish-repatriation reading is disputed (text mentions only Mesopotamian sanctuaries) and that the text follows older Babylonian royal tradition.
- Administration: Wood's Oxford thesis review says most scholars want a "less extreme" Achaemenid-continuity reading for the Seleukids; Aperghis argues for substantial Achaemenid precedent in finances. These two are the pair.
- Abbasid route: a quotation on the Barmakids page questions a Khurasan-mediated transfer of Indian learning.
- No source opened for Christianity directly; the Magi/Christianity strand is unevidenced here.
- Evidence base is one-sided toward pointers: most of the Abbasid, Ottoman and Urdu strands rest on Wikipedia.
