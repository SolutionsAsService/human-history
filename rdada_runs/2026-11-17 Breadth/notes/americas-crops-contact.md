# americas-crops-contact: Americas crops, metals, voyages and the Columbian Exchange

## Ask
"Americas: maize domestication and spread (Matsuoka 2002, Kistler 2018, Piperno), Andean-Mesoamerican metallurgy contact (Hosler), Polynesian-Native American contact (Ioannidis 2020 aDNA and the sweet potato), Norse contact in Newfoundland (L'Anse aux Meadows 1021 CE dendro date, Kuitems 2022), the Columbian Exchange (Crosby; primary texts such as Columbus's letters if open). State what is documented, inferred and disputed for each, and quote the main sceptical papers."

## Cited findings
Maize
- Single domestication in southern Mexico c. 9,000 years ago, inferred from 99 microsatellites on 264 plants (inferred) — [Matsuoka et al. 2002, PNAS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC122905/)
- Maize starch and phytoliths at Xihuatoxtla shelter, Balsas valley, by 8,700 cal BP (documented, one site) — [Piperno et al. 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2664021/); site excavation and radiocarbon context — [Ranere et al. 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2664064/)
- Maize in South America by ~6500 BP, partially domesticated, secondary improvement in the SW Amazon (inferred; abstract only) — [Kistler et al. 2018 (Warwick WRAP)](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/110238/)
- Paredones (Peru) maize ~6700-5000 cal BP of Mesoamerican origin (inferred) — [Vallebueno-Estrada et al. 2023, eLife](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10112886/)

Metallurgy
- Hosler: West Mexican metallurgy c. 700 CE introduced from northern South America along the Pacific coast, "an unambiguous case of technology transfer" (inferred; proponent's own abstract) — [Hosler 2014](https://link.springer.com/rwe/10.1007/978-1-4614-9017-3_14)

Polynesia and America
- Native American ancestry in eastern Polynesia, one contact event c. 1150-1230 CE (debated) — [Ioannidis et al. 2020, Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-020-2487-2). The paper itself says critics hold the sweet potato dispersal need not be human-mediated; its Rapa Nui estimate (1380 CE) is later and may be inflated by later Chilean admixture (PMC full text).
- Sweet potato in Central Polynesia by 1200-1300 CE, vector contested (debated) — [Denham 2013 commentary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3568348/)

Norse
- Norse-cut wood at L'Anse aux Meadows felled 1021 CE, three trees, via the 993 CE radiocarbon spike (documented for the site) — [Kuitems et al. 2022, Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03972-8)

Columbian Exchange and Columbus
- Overview of exchange of diseases, crops, technologies after 1492 (context) — [Nunn and Qian 2010](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jep.24.2.163)
- Santangel letter, 15 Feb 1493: promise of spice and cotton for the Crown (inferred intent, not a shipment record) — [EMS edition](https://www.ems.kcl.ac.uk/content/etext/e022.html)
- Diario, 16 Oct 1492: "panizo" sown year-round (debated: not stated as maize; text is an abstract) — [EMS Diario](https://www.ems.kcl.ac.uk/content/etext/e019.html)

Documented / inferred / disputed summary
- Maize: Mexican origin documented archaeologically (8,700 BP); single-origin and the route south inferred from genetics; Kistler adds a partially domesticated southward population (refinement, not refutation).
- Metallurgy: transfer from the south asserted by Hosler; route and mechanism inferred; no independent doubter opened.
- Polynesia: contact c. 1200 CE disputed in who sailed; sweet potato presence by 1200-1300 CE is archaeological, human vector debated.
- Norse: 1021 CE at one site documented; wider activity and Indigenous exchange unestablished.
- Columbian Exchange: broad fact is context; Columbus texts opened show intent and an ambiguous crop word.

## Sceptical and alternative pass
- Polynesia: [Matisoo-Smith and Gosling 2020](https://theconversation.com/did-ancient-americans-settle-in-polynesia-the-evidence-doesnt-stack-up-142383) (Polynesian voyagers, not Native Americans; ancient Rapa Nui DNA shows no admixture). Ioannidis's own text records critics of human dispersal of the sweet potato.
- Norse: the paper's own caution (future research on wider Norse activity; Greenlandic genomes show no gene exchange).
- Maize: Kistler's partially domesticated lineage qualifies Matsuoka's single-trajectory model; no paper arguing multiple origins was opened.
- Not found/opened: independent sceptic of Hosler's Ecuador-West Mexico thesis; Munoz-Rodriguez 2018 (sweet potato reached Polynesia without humans), recalled, not fetched; Roullier 2013 genetic paper itself.
- One-sided warning: the metallurgy and Columbian Exchange strands have support only.
