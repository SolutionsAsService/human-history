# east-asia-sinosphere: East Asia and the Sinosphere (thread)

## Ask
East Asia: how Chinese writing, Buddhism, Confucian learning and technologies reached Korea, Japan and Vietnam. Primary: Nihon Shoki entry on the arrival of Buddhism (538/552), Kojiki preface, Samguk sagi/Samguk yusa passages, Hunminjeongeum (Hangul, 1446) preface, Jikji (1377, earliest extant metal-type book). Also paper and printing to Korea and gunpowder and compass spread (Needham; sceptical sources). Quote each at its locus with composition date and basis, and the scholarly caution.

Every quote in `east-asia-sinosphere.candidates.json` (21 candidates, all schema-valid) was confirmed with `check_quote.py` against the cited URL before it was recorded. Dates of composition come from sources I opened, named in each `text.composition.basis`. Witness dates (earliest manuscripts) were not established from any source I opened and are marked so.

## Cited findings
- Nihon Shoki, Kimmei 13th year: Paekche's king sends a Shaka image and sutras with a memorial saying "My law shall spread to the East"; a pestilence follows and the image is thrown into the Naniha canal. Aston prints the year as A.D. 552. — [Aston 1896, Nihongi vol. 2 (archive.org)](https://archive.org/stream/nihongi2asto/nihongi2asto_djvu.txt)
- Nihon Shoki composition: "the Nihon shoki compiled in 720". — [Yoshida 2003, JJRS 30/1-2](https://nirc.nanzan-u.ac.jp/journal/6/article/1101/pdf/download)
- Nihon Shoki, Suiko 18th year: the Koguryo priest Tam-chhi (Damjing) is "skilled in preparing painters' colours, paper, and ink" and made mills. — [Aston 1896](https://archive.org/stream/nihongi2asto/nihongi2asto_djvu.txt)
- Aston's Introduction: Wani (Wang-in) was the first positive datum for Chinese writing in Japan, A.D. 405, for which the Nihongi gives an erroneous date corresponding to A.D. 285 (a 120-year cycle error). — [Aston 1896, Nihongi vol. 1](https://archive.org/stream/nihongi1asto/nihongi1asto_djvu.txt)
- Kojiki preface, memorial dated the 28th day of the 1st month of Wado 5 (712 by Chamberlain's reckoning, Wado 4 = Nov. 711): Yasumaro says it was hard to write the old language in characters and so mixed phonetic and ideographic use. — [Chamberlain 1882, Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Kojiki_(Chamberlain,_1882)/Preface)
- Kojiki Sect. CX (Ojin): Wani-kishi sent from Kudara with the Analects (10 vols) and the Thousand Character Essay (1 vol.), plus a smith and a weaver; Chamberlain notes the Nihongi mention only "various classics". — [Chamberlain 1882, Sect. 110](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Kojiki_(Chamberlain,_1882)/Section_110)
- Samguk sagi, Goguryeo, Sosurim year 2 (372): Former Qin sends the monk Sundo with images and scriptures; a Taehak (academy) is founded; year 5 the first temples, "the beginning of Buddhism in Haedong". — [juan 18](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/三國史記/卷18)
- Samguk sagi, Silla, Beopheung year 15: Buddhism "begins to be practised"; the monk Mukhoja had come from Goguryeo earlier. — [juan 4](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/三國史記/卷04)
- Samguk sagi, Baekje, Chimnyu year 1 (384): the Hu monk Maranant'a comes from Jin; "Buddhism began from here". — [juan 24](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/三國史記/卷24)
- Samguk sagi compilation: ordered by King Injong (r. 1122-1146), by Kim Busik; cites no Japanese chronicles, draws on Chinese dynastic histories. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samguk_sagi)
- Hunminjeongeum Haerye preface: "國之語音。異乎中國。與文字不相流通" (Korean speech differs from China's and does not fit its characters); the postface of Jeong Inji is dated the first ten days of the 9th month, Zhengtong 11 (1446), and says Chinese script is borrowed to write foreign speech, mentioning Silla's Seol Chong's idu. — [Korean Wikisource](https://ko.wikisource.org/wiki/훈민정음)
- Jikji imprint 宣光七年丁巳七月日淸州牧外興德寺鑄字印施 (1377); the only extant movable metal type print is Jikji vol. 2, at the BnF. — [Review of Korean Studies 29(1), 2026, doi 10.25024/review.2026.29.1.013](https://www.accesson.kr/rks/assets/pdf/59324/journal-29-1-351.pdf)
- BnF: "Jikji is the oldest known book in the world printed with movable metal type." — [BnF](https://www.bnf.fr/en/jikji-treasure-world-printing)
- Hou Hanshu 76: Xi Guang (Jiaozhi) and Ren Yan (Jiuzhen) taught rites, iron-tool ploughing and marriage customs; "Chinese customs in Lingnan began with these two". Composition more than 200 years after the Han fell (Wikipedia). — [Wikisource](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/後漢書/卷76)
- Toan thu (Ngo Si Lien, 1479 original edition): Vietnam's learning "began with the Shi King (Shi Xie)". — [Wikisource](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/大越史記全書/外紀卷之三)
- Paper reached Korea by trade in Sosurim's reign; when papermaking itself arrived is "not certain". — [Hanji paper, KFS Journal 20(3), 2009](https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO200919463953268.pdf)
- Gunpowder reached Goryeo via the merchant Li Yuan and Choe Museon; an office was set up in 1377. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%27oe_Mus%C5%8Fn)

## Sceptical and alternative pass
- **Buddhism's date in Japan.** Yoshida (2003) is the strongest sceptic: the Nihon Shoki passage depends on a Golden Light Sutra version translated into Chinese only in 703; 552 comes from a mappo calculation; 538 rests on the Gangoji engi and the Jogu Shotoku hootei setsu, later than the Nihon Shoki; "we have no basis for pinning down a specific date". Yoshida (2015) judges the Gangoji garan engi (claiming 747) a later forgery. Both are recorded with stance `contradicts` and `directness: debated`. They accept transmission from Paekche in the mid-6th century in general.
- **Writing in Japan.** Aston's own chronology note (285 versus 405, a two-cycle error) is the caution for the Wani strand; Chamberlain's footnote notes the Nihongi's vaguer "various classics". The Thousand Character Essay's later date is a standard objection, but I did not open a source for it, so it is not asserted.
- **Samguk sagi.** The Buddhism entries are 12th-century retrospectives of 4th-6th century events, and the work leans on Chinese histories. Marked `inferred`, not `documented`. Samguk yusa was not read (see Gaps).
- **Vietnam.** The only sceptical Vietnam sources I could reach were blocked or weak (see Gaps), so the Vietnam strands rest on two retrospective narratives (Fan Ye, Ngo Si Lien) and are `inferred` and `debated`. This is one-sided; the brief needs a Holmgren or Whitmore style source opened.
- **Paper, printing, gunpowder, compass.** Kang (2022) rebuts Needham's claim of a Chinese origin for the matchlock; this is a rebuttal of one Needham claim only. The Wikipedia compass article states diffusion is disputed. The Choe Museon account is tertiary. Needham's own volumes and Tsien's paper and printing volume were not opened. Jikji: the 1377 imprint is solid for the extant item, but the transfer of movable type from China to Korea is not documented by any source I opened (the lost Sangjeong gogeum yemun of 1234 and Bi Sheng's clay type came up only in search snippets, so they are not asserted).
- Nothing found that disputes the Hangul preface text itself; the preface documents rejection of an imported script, not its arrival.
