# africa-bantu-sahara: Bantu expansion, Nok iron, trans-Saharan trade and Islam, Periplus, Aksum

## Ask
1. Africa: what do linguistics, ancient DNA and archaeology show about the Bantu expansion (dates, routes, Lipson et al. 2020, Patin et al. 2017, Bostoen's work) and what is disputed? Iron working in West Africa (Nok) and its independence or diffusion?
2. Primary texts on trans-Saharan trade and Islam in West Africa (al-Bakri 1068, Ibn Battuta on Mali 1352, Ibn Khaldun), the Periplus of the Erythraean Sea on the East African coast and Aksum's adoption of Christianity (Ezana inscriptions; Rufinus). Quote each at its locus with composition date and basis.

## Cited findings
All 20 quotes were confirmed with check_quote.py (final re-run: 20 of 20 found).

Bantu expansion
- Shum Laka DNA (c. 8000 and 3000 years old) is unlike most Niger-Congo speakers today; the authors say it neither supports nor contradicts a Grassfields origin. — [Lipson et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8386425/)
- Present-day genomes favour a "late-split": south through the rainforest, then east and south. — [Patin et al. 2017 (Porto repository copy)](https://repositorio-aberto.up.pt/bitstream/10216/109265/1/Patin2017Science.pdf)
- Language phylogeny: expansion from c. 5,000 years ago following savannah corridors. — [Grollemund et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4629331/)
- Rival language analysis: rainforest route at 4,420 BP (4,040-5,000), before the Sangha corridor opened. — [Koile et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9372543/)
- Archaeology fits a migration across the rainforest in about the first millennium BCE, but "much uncertainty persists". — [Bostoen & Coutros, chapter 1](https://backoffice.biblio.ugent.be/download/01K0HCDZ1Y0VS3PNG4QASJCTQK/01K21Y5TTXTZ70X0SKDSM3ZHXF)
- 1149 radiocarbon dates show a collapse between 400 and 600 CE; "spread-over-spread" model. — [Seidensticker et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7880602/)

Iron and Nok
- Earliest West and West-Central African smelting sites cannot be dated more closely than 840-420 BCE; dates before 800 BCE would favour invention. — [Vansina 2006](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/41F1892C8476F077008D2C08AAA4085E/S0361541300004101a.pdf/div-class-title-linguistic-evidence-for-the-introduction-of-ironworking-into-bantu-speaking-africa-div.pdf)
- Nok furnace charcoal at Intini, 519-410 BCE; McIntosh favours transmission across the Sahara (context only). — [Archaeology magazine 2011](https://archive.archaeology.org/1107/features/nok_nigeria_africa_terracotta.html)
- Overview of the dispute, including the Oboui dates (context only). — [Wikipedia, Iron metallurgy in Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_metallurgy_in_Africa)

Primary texts
- al-Bakri (composed 1068): Muslim town with twelve mosques; Muslim ministers at the king's court; salt levy; gold of Ghiyaru. Two candidates. — [Levtzion & Hopkins extract (AUI handout)](https://my.aui.ma/ICS/icsfs/Arab_Sources_on_Medieval_West_Africa.pdf?target=b1046592-a60d-4640-b35a-661239617e49)
- Ibn Battuta (in Mali June 1352 to Feb 1353; dictation finished 9 Dec 1355): observant Muslims in Mali; Taghaza salt town. Two candidates. — [LibreTexts, Gibb translation](https://human.libretexts.org/Courses/The_Westminster_Schools/Ibn_Battuta's_Travels_in_Africa)
- Ibn Khaldun (Muqaddimah drafted to Nov 1377): Ghana belongs to the Mali ruler; the only gold mine he knows is in the Sudan. Two candidates. — [Rosenthal translation, PDF copy](https://traditionalhikma.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/The-Muqaddimah-by-Ibn-Khaldun-Translated-by-Franz-Rosenthal.pdf)
- Periplus, sections 15-17: Rhapta, Muza's Arab captains who intermarry; composed mid-1st century CE (Schoff: c. 60, before 70). — [Schoff 1912, Internet Archive OCR](https://archive.org/stream/periplusoferythr00scho/periplusoferythr00scho_djvu.txt)
- Socrates Scholasticus 1.19 retells the Frumentius story and names Rufinus as source. — [New Advent](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/26011.htm)
- Ezana's Greek inscription, "In the faith of God and the Power of the Father...", as quoted by a secondary article; composition 333-360 on its basis. — [Rukuni 2021](https://scielo.org.za/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S2074-77052021000100001)

## Sceptical and alternative pass
- Bantu: Koile 2022 (rainforest route) against the savannah-corridor model; Seidensticker 2021 against a continuous expansion; Bostoen & Coutros list the open questions and note Clist and others who read the radiocarbon hiatus as a research gap (not opened).
- Nok iron: Vansina frames independence versus borrowing as unresolved on 840-420 BCE dates; the magazine and Wikipedia pages carry the diffusionist (McIntosh) and Oboui (Clist vs Holl) disputes. Nothing primary on Nok dating was opened, so the Nok iron chronology rests on a popular source plus Vansina and is the weakest part of this brief.
- Trans-Saharan texts: the Koumbi Saleh page notes the al-Bakri town is identified by inference only. Ibn Khaldun wrote decades after Mali's peak.
- Aksum: Rukuni reports doubts (Lord of Heaven read as pagan or Judaic; two Ezanas; Constantius's letter) and partly rebuts them.
- Only the Aksum and Nok threads contain real disagreement among sources; the medieval Arabic texts were read for what they say, not tested against archaeology.
