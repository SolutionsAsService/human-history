# mongol-plague-exchange: Mongol-era exchange and the Black Death

## Ask
Mongol-era exchange and the Black Death: primary accounts (Rubruck's journey, Marco Polo, Rashid al-Din on Mongol-era translation, Gabriele de' Mussi on the plague at Caffa 1346, Ibn al-Wardi on the plague), ancient-DNA studies of Yersinia pestis (Spyrou 2019, 2022), and what is disputed about the route of the Black Death. Quote primary passages at their loci with composition date and basis, and the strongest scholarly caution on the Caffa story.

## Cited findings
All 20 quotes were confirmed with check_quote.py against the cited URL on 2026-11-17.
- De' Mussi: dying Tartars "lobbed into the city" corpses placed in catapults; written 1348 or early 1349 (presumed), sole copy in a c. 1367 compilation — [Wheelis 2002](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/eid/article/8/9/01-0536_article)
- De' Mussi marks the move "from east to west" to what he saw himself; deeds place him in Piacenza through mid-1346 — [Wheelis 2002](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/eid/article/8/9/01-0536_article)
- Wheelis judges the attack plausible, consistent with the technology of the time — same page
- Ibn al-Wardi (d. 18 March 1349): plague "began in the land of darkness", then China, India, Sind, Transoxiana, Persia, Crimea — [Dols translation](https://archive.org/download/michaeldolstr.ibnalwardiontheplague1974/Michael%20Dols%20tr.%20Ibn%20al-Wardi%20on%20the%20Plague%201974.pdf)
- Polo: a German and a Nestorian in his party built mangonels for Kublai at Saianfu — [Yule-Cordier vol. 2](https://archive.org/download/thetravelsofmarc12410gut/12410-8.txt)
- Rubruck (report 1255): a Parisian goldsmith at Karakorum; Nestorians and Saracens mixed in Uighur towns — [Rockhill](https://archive.org/stream/journeyofwilliam00will/journeyofwilliam00will_djvu.txt)
- Rashid al-Din (Compendium composed 1300-10): Kashmiri monk and Chinese, Uighur, Qipchaq scholars as informants; Tansuq-nama on Chinese medicine — [Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/jame%CA%BF-al-tawari%E1%B8%B5/); translation into Chinese claimed only in popular [Aramco World](https://archive.aramcoworld.com/issue/198101/a.history.of.the.world.htm)
- Spyrou 2022: ancient Y. pestis from Issyk-Kul tombstones dated 1338-39 support a central Eurasian source — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-04800-3)
- Spyrou 2019: 34 genomes support one entry via eastern Europe; source and route "remain hypothetical" — [Nat Commun](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-12154-0)
- Bauch and Büntgen 2025: Golden Horde grain imported 1347 as the vector — [Commun Earth Environ](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-025-02964-0)
- Namouchi 2018: possible fur-trade routes and repeated introductions — [PNAS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6294933/)

## Sceptical and alternative pass
- Caffa: Zanders ([HN1](https://www.the-trench.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/HN001-de-Mussi-and-the-siege-of-Caffa-Full.pdf), [HN2](https://www.the-trench.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/HN002-Catapulting-Corpses-Full.pdf)) is the strongest caution I opened: de' Mussi is the only source, was not at Caffa, no contemporary text corroborates the catapulting, and whole-carcass catapult stories always rest on one non-eyewitness. These are not peer-reviewed working papers; the Wheelis paper is peer reviewed but argues only plausibility. Hannah Barker's Speculum 2021 article, which argues plague reached Europe via Tana and grain, was not opened (see Gaps).
- Footnote in Wheelis: the machines were trebuchets, not catapults (stone-throwing power needed).
- Polo's Saianfu story: Yule's own note says the chronology cannot work (siege ended 1273, Polos arrived after 1274), so the technology-transfer claim is debated.
- Mongol conquests and plague: Brack, Biran and Amitai 2024 find no evidence of plague at Baghdad 1258 or carried by Mongols; Fancy and Green 2024 reply with a "focalisation" scenario. Disputed.
- Route: Spyrou 2019/2022 (Central Asia/eastern Europe entry), Bauch and Büntgen 2025 (grain trade), Namouchi 2018 (fur routes). The Caffa-to-Genoa step is the one source none of the ancient DNA tests directly.
- Roosen and Curtis 2018 caution that route maps built on Biraben's uncited data are weak.
- Alternative-claim pass: apart from Namouchi and Bauch, I found no proponent of a local European origin in the pages opened.
- Brief is thin on Rashid al-Din's own words: only secondary and one popular source.
