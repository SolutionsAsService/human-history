# writing-systems-breakdown: Writing thread, breakdown by script family

## Ask
"Break the writing thread into documented adaptations: Ugaritic alphabetic cuneiform (c. 1400 BCE), Linear A to Linear B and Cypriot syllabary, runes and their possible Italic origin, Glagolitic and Cyrillic (Life of Constantine/Cyril, Vita Methodii; Chernorizets Hrabar on the alphabet), Armenian and Georgian (Koriun), Ge'ez, Arabic script from Nabataean, Hangul (Hunminjeongeum), Japanese kana from kanji." / "Quote primary passages or inscriptions at their loci with date and basis, and the dispute where one exists (e.g. runic origin)."

## Cited findings
- Ugaritic: alphabetic script in cuneiform wedges, emerging c. 1400 or 1300 BCE; hypotheses of Akkadian-cuneiform or Proto-Sinaitic inspiration, neither documented — [Wikipedia, Ugaritic alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugaritic_alphabet)
- A 15th-century BCE Egyptian ostracon is called the oldest known abecedary, earlier than Ugarit's — [Leiden news on Haring](https://universiteitleiden.nl/en/news/2015/11/earliest-known-alphabetic-word-list-discovered)
- Linear A/B: Evans (1921) says Class A differs from Class B in half its characters; Class B was the script of the Knossos palace — [Evans, Palace of Minos I, Internet Archive](https://archive.org/download/cu31924081667796/cu31924081667796_djvu.txt)
- Linear B adapted from Linear A, deciphered 1952 by Ventris — [Wikipedia, Linear B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_B); Cypriot syllabary "is thought" derived from Cypro-Minoan — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cypriot_syllabary)
- Runes: Tacitus Germania 10 (twigs "notis quibusdam discretos", signs not letters) — [Latin Library](https://www.thelatinlibrary.com/tacitus/tac.ger.shtml); Venantius Fortunatus "Barbara fraxineis pingatur RUNA tabellis", as quoted by Gibbon — [CCEL](https://ccel.org/g/gibbon/decline/volume1/nt9/016.htm); Kylver stone c. 400 CE with complete futhark — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kylver_Stone)
- Slavic: Hrabar says Slavs used "strokes and incisions" before Roman/Greek letters were adapted until Cyril — [Wikipedia, Chernorizets Hrabar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernorizets_Hrabar); Life of Constantine: God "made letters in your language appear now in our times" (as quoted in Ivanova 2020) — [White Rose eprint](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/177959/1/slaveasteurorev2.98.3.0434.pdf)
- Armenian and Georgian (Koriwn, Norehad 1964): Daniel's letters; Mashtots creates the Armenian alphabet; goes to Georgia to make a Georgian alphabet — [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/download/LifeOfMashtots/Koriwn_1964_Life_of_Mashtots_djvu.txt). Date: last dated event in the Life is 443 (Terian in [Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/koriwn/))
- Arabic: Namara epitaph, 328 CE, Nabataean script (Bellamy translation) — [Islamic Awareness](https://islamic-awareness.org/history/islam/inscriptions/namarah); Hoyland dates it to 328 — [PDF](https://almuslih.org/wp-content/uploads/Library/Hoyland,%20R%20-%20Birth%20of%20Arabic%20in%20Stone.pdf)
- Ge'ez: derived from Ancient South Arabian; vocalised by Ezana's time (4th c.) — [Wikipedia, Ge'ez script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ge%CA%BDez_script); Ezana Stone text — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezana_Stone)
- Hangul: Sejong's preface and Jeong Inji's postface (28 letters, winter 1443) — [Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Hunminjeongeum)
- Kana: Tosa Nikki opening (935), kana in a woman's voice — [OSU reader](https://cjp.asc.ohio-state.edu/books/tosa-nikki/pages/1); hiragana via man'yogana from kanji — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiragana)

## Sceptical and alternative pass
- Runic origin: [Elder Futhark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elder_Futhark) says Old Italic or Latin is the usual view but "there is no derivation so straightforward as to convince most scholars"; Greek is excluded by the Vimose date; Etruscan, Raetic and Latin candidates all open. Tacitus and Fortunatus only show that signs/"runa" existed, not an origin. One search result describes a Punic thesis as the best-explained (unverified; the academia.edu page did not open).
- Georgian: Koriwn's tradition is contested ([Georgian scripts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_scripts) quoting Rayfield).
- Hrabar: dominant reading vs the reading as a defence of Glagolitic against Cyrillic; 38 letters in manuscripts vs 36 in Glagolitic.
- Ge'ez: origin region (Eritrea, Ethiopia, Yemen) debated.
- Cypriot: the Cypro-Minoan link is hedged. Ugaritic: earlier Egyptian abecedary shows alphabetic order predates Ugarit's tablets.
- No sceptical source found for Namara, Hangul or kana beyond hedges in the pages cited.
