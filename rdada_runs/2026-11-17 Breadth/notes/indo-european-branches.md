# indo-european-branches: Indo-European branch evidence (primary texts)

## Ask
Break the Indo-European thread into branch evidence: Hittite and Luwian texts (earliest Anatolian IE attestation; the Anitta text), Mycenaean Linear B as the earliest Greek, Tocharian manuscripts of the Tarim Basin, Gaulish inscriptions, Old Persian and Vedic, the earliest Germanic inscriptions, Armenian (Koriun). Quote primary passages or inscriptions at their loci with date and basis, and what the earliest attestation shows about spread (and does not).

## Cited findings
All 21 quotes were confirmed with check_quote.py on 2026-11-17 against the URL given. Dates are those the page states; where I give a range it is marked as mine in the candidate.
- Anatolian: opening line of the Anitta text, Hittite transliteration with analysis, and a commentary note that Anitta and Pithana have non-IE names while the god Siu is IE — [Hittite Online Lesson 1](https://lrc.la.utexas.edu/eieol/hitol/10)
- Anitta text dated to 1700 BCE, possibly first composed in Akkadian at Kanesh (Archi); oldest text in any IE language — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anitta_text)
- Earliest Hittite: Kültepe names and loanwords, called 19th c. BCE on one page and 20th c. BCE on another (Hittite_language page, quote checked) — [Anatolian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolian_languages)
- Luwian hieroglyphs on seals from early 2nd millennium BCE — [Luwian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luwian_language)
- Greek: Pylos Ta 641 (ti-ri-po-de), the Pylos archive destroyed at start of LH IIIC, in Ventris and Chadwick (2nd ed. 1973, archive.org OCR) — [Documents in Mycenaean Greek](https://archive.org/download/MichaelVentrisJohnChadwickDocumentsInMYCENAEANGREEKIlovepdfCompressed/Michael_Ventris,__John_Chadwick]_Documents_in_MYCENAEAN%20GREEK-ilovepdf-compressed_djvu.txt)
- Tocharian: Mair says there is no direct evidence of Tocharians in the Tarim before the medieval texts — [Mair 2013](https://cetom.univie.ac.at/download/conference_2013/Mair.pdf); Peyrot's edition of archaic-language fragment THT 3597 — [Peyrot 2010](https://ir.library.osaka-u.ac.jp/repo/ouka/all/26139/sial25-143.pdf)
- Gaulish: Coligny plaque late 2nd c. CE — [Coligny calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coligny_calendar); Lezoux plate line — [Lezoux Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lezoux_Plate)
- Germanic: Vimose comb c. 160 CE — [Vimose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vimose_inscriptions); Negau B possibly 350–300 BCE — [Negau helmets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negau_helmets); Gallehus, early 5th c. — [Gallehus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Horns_of_Gallehus)
- Armenian: Koriun on the invention of the letters — [Life of Mashtots](https://www.vehi.net/istoriya/armenia/korun/english/02.html)
- Vedic: Rigveda 1.1.1 — [rigveda-online](https://rigveda-online.github.io/1/1.html); date 1500–1000 BCE per [Wikipedia Rigveda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigveda) (checked, cited in limits)
- Indo-Aryan names in the Near East: Mitanni treaty gods, Kikkuli — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_superstrate_in_Mitanni)
- Dispute on the spread: Heggarty et al. 2023 abstract — [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1126/science.abg0818), DOI 10.1126/science.abg0818

## Sceptical and alternative pass
- Negau: Smith's caution that teiva may be a second name (page-reported); date unclear.
- Tocharian: Mair's own concession of no pre-medieval direct evidence; the Afanasievo link is inference.
- Origin: Heggarty 2023 (root c. 8120 BP, hybrid) against a pure steppe model; only the abstract was read. Anatolian page presents both views.
- Everything on Wikipedia is secondary; underlying citations (Archi, Teržan 2012, Seebold 1994, Thieme 1960) were not opened. Several inscriptions (Coligny, Lezoux, Vimose, Negau, Gallehus) are therefore quoted via a Wikipedia transcription, not a corpus edition (RIG, Rundata).
- Weakest points: the Greek and Hittite material is better covered than Celtic, Germanic and Armenian. The Kikkuli and Mitanni candidates are summaries.
- Old Persian: Behistun was read earlier; I did not make a candidate. Wikipedia Old_Persian (read) dates it to 522 BCE.
- The English translation of Koriun on vehi.net names no translator; Peyrot's THT 3597 quote is a translation.
