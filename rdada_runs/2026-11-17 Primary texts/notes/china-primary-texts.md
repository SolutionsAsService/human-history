# china-primary-texts: Chinese primary texts on Silk Road contact

## Ask
Quote Chinese primary sources for contact and transmission along the Silk Road (Shiji 123; Hou Hanshu on Prince Ying of Chu, 65 CE; Cai Lun and paper, 105 CE; Faxian or Xuanzang), with composition ranges, earliest witnesses and textual cautions (Ming-ti dream).

## Cited findings
All Chinese quotes were confirmed with check_quote.py on the page named, 2026-11-17.
- Zhang Qian saw Shu cloth and Qiong canes in Daxia, bought via Shendu: [Shiji 123, zh.wikisource](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%8F%B2%E8%A8%98/%E5%8D%B7123)
- Alfalfa and grapes planted by the emperor after envoys brought seed from Dayuan (same page).
- Prince Ying "learned fu-tu fasting and sacrifices"; the Yongping 8 (65 CE) edict names fu-tu, upasaka and sramana: [Hou Hanshu 42](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%BE%8C%E6%BC%A2%E6%9B%B8/%E5%8D%B742)
- Hou Hanshu 88 Tianzhu section: Buddha practice, Ming dream as "shi chuan" (tradition), Gan Ying turned back (97 CE): [Hou Hanshu 88](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%BE%8C%E6%BC%A2%E6%9B%B8/%E5%8D%B788); Hirth's English: [China and the Roman Orient](https://archive.org/stream/chinaandromanor01hirtgoog/chinaandromanor01hirtgoog_djvu.txt)
- Cai Lun's paper of bark, hemp, rags, nets, presented 105 CE: [Hou Hanshu 78](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%BE%8C%E6%BC%A2%E6%9B%B8/%E5%8D%B778) (page is in simplified characters).
- Faxian at Khotan and Java: [Legge 1886](https://archive.org/stream/recordofbuddhist00jame/recordofbuddhist00jame_djvu.txt); Xuanzang's Khotan silkworm legend: [Beal 1884 vol. 2](https://archive.org/stream/siyukibuddhistre02hsuoft/siyukibuddhistre02hsuoft_djvu.txt)

Dates: Shiji complete by ca. 91 BCE (Letter to Ren An), earliest extant copy 420-589 CE, only two pre-Tang fragments ([Wikipedia Shiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiji)). Hou Hanshu compiled by Fan Ye et al. in the 5th century ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_the_Later_Han)); I narrowed the range to 420-445 from memory of Liu Song/Fan Ye, not shown on the page. Faxian wrote c. 414; Xuanzang completed 646. Shiji start year -109 is my reading of Sima Tan's death 110 BCE.

## Sceptical and alternative pass
- Ming-ti dream: Pelliot (T'oung Pao 1919/1920) calls the dream and embassy legendary, citing Maspero BEFEO X (1910). Fan Ye himself introduces it as hearsay ("shi chuan"). Chu Ying's 65 CE edict is the earliest datable item, but it is retold by a 5th-century compiler. A web search summarised Zürcher as treating the 65 CE episode as the earliest hard evidence and Ch'en as arguing earlier presence; I did not open those and do not cite them.
- Cai Lun: single ~300-character biography; excavated paper at Fangmatan (179-141 BCE per History of paper page) predates him, so he is the improver/promoter. Fangmatan dating differs across sources; Baqiao was seen only in search snippets and is not cited.
- The Shiji account of Zhang Qian rests on his report; the Hou Hanshu West Regions chapter on Ban Yong's report. The Khotan silk story is a legend heard c. 644.
- Wikipedia pages are tertiary and used only for dates and cautions.
