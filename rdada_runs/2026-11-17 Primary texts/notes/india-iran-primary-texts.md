# india-iran-primary-texts: Primary texts for Indo-Iranian and Indian contact

## Ask
1. "Quote the primary evidence for Indo-Iranian and Indian contact: the Hittite-Mitanni treaty (c. 1380 BCE) invoking Mitra, Varuna, Indra and the Nasatyas; the Rigveda (hymns to Indra, Mitra-Varuna) in an opened translation; Yasna 30 from the Avesta; the Edicts of Ashoka (Rock Edict 13 naming Greek kings; the Greek and Aramaic inscriptions at Kandahar); the Behistun inscription."
2. "Give composition ranges with basis (Rigveda and Avesta dates are scholarly ranges, quote the basis), the earliest witness, and the edition or translation read. Add the strongest scholarly caution on Indo-Iranian dating."

24 candidates are in `india-iran-primary-texts.candidates.json`. Every quote was confirmed with `check_quote.py` against the URL recorded (final pass: 24 of 24 found). The validator's only complaint is the "verifier marker" that a later stage adds.

## Cited findings

### Hittite-Mitanni treaty (Suppiluliuma I and Shattiwaza)
- The oath list includes "the Mitra -gods, the Varuna -gods, Indra , the Nasatya -gods", in an English version labelled Beckman, among Storm-god, Ea, Anu, Enlil and others — [Heritage Institute excerpts](https://www.heritageinstitute.com/zoroastrianism/ranghaya/suppiluliuma_shattiwaza_treaty.htm)
- Cornil's French translation gives line 56 as "les divinités Uruwanasse, Indar, les divinités Nasattijanna", after "Mitrassil" in line 55. He dates Suppiluliuma 1380-1346 and bases it on KBo I 1-2 and KUB III 1 with Weidner's transliteration — [Cornil PDF](https://sites.unipa.it/dipstdir/portale/pub/riviste/CORNIL.pdf)
- Gentile dates the treaty "ca. 1380 BC" and lists it with Amarna letters, Kikkuli and Nuzi as the sources for Aryan names in Mitanni — [Gentile 2019](https://www.diacronia.ro/indexing/details/A31188/pdf)
- Kikkuli's text opens "Thus speaks Kikkuli, master horse trainer of the land of Mitanni"; Wikipedia says it carries Indo-Aryan numeral compounds and puts Kikkuli c. 1400 BC (copies: Late Hittite 13th c. and two contemporary Middle Hittite) — [Wikipedia: Kikkuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kikkuli)
- Burrow (1973 extract): the Near Eastern Aryan vestiges "are to be connected specifically with Indo-Aryan, and not with Iranian" — [JRAS](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-royal-asiatic-society/article/protoindoaryans/D33CF44DA7BA7B2D3E5FF36F667D4114)

### Rigveda (Griffith, archive.org OCR)
- RV 2.12.3 (Indra): "Who slew the dragon, freed the seven rivers, and drove the kine forth from the cave of Vala" — [Griffith vol. I, 1889](https://archive.org/stream/hymnsrigveda02grifgoog/hymnsrigveda02grifgoog_djvu.txt)
- RV 5.63.1 (Mitra-Varuna): "Guardians of Order, ye whose laws are ever true, in the sublimest heaven your chariot ye ascend" — [Griffith vol. II, 1890](https://archive.org/stream/hymnsrigveda00grifgoog/hymnsrigveda00grifgoog_djvu.txt)
- RV 10.125.1 (Mitra-Varuna, Indra, Agni, Asvins) is set beside the treaty gods by Gentile, citing Dumezil 1952 and the Jamison-Brereton 2014 translation — [Gentile 2019](https://www.diacronia.ro/indexing/details/A31188/pdf)
- Dating basis: Wikipedia quotes Jamison and Brereton that dating "has been and is likely to remain a matter of contention", with proposals "inferred from the style and the content within the hymns themselves"; bulk in the second half of the 2nd millennium BCE; earliest surviving manuscripts c. 1040 CE (Nepal), after oral transmission — [Wikipedia: Rigveda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigveda). Livius gives 1500-1200 BCE with no sources cited in the text read — [Livius: Avesta](https://www.livius.org/sources/content/avesta)

### Yasna 30
- Mills, SBE 31 (1887), Y30.3: "Thus are the primeval spirits who as a pair ..." and Y30.6: "the Demon-gods (and they who give them worship) can make no righteous choice" — [archive.org](https://archive.org/stream/zendavesta03darm/zendavesta03darm_djvu.txt). Title page of the same scan confirms Mills, Clarendon Press 1887.
- Mills's own dating: "the Gathas may possibly have been composed as early as about 1500 B.C." and perhaps as late as 900-1200 B.C., "in view of the established age of the Rig-veda", so Gathic dating is derived from a Vedic estimate.
- Wikipedia: Gathas plausible 1500-900 BCE, c. 1000 most likely; written transmission only from the Sasanian period (opened, not made a candidate; see Gaps). Livius gives Zarathustra 1400-1200 BCE.
- A second translation (Boyce, via Livius, "there are two primal Spirits, twins renowned to be in conflict") was opened and checked but not recorded, to keep the list within range.

### Ashoka
- RE II (Kalsi): "the Yona king named Antiyoga, and the other kings who are the neighbours of this Antiyoga" — [Hultzsch](https://archive.org/download/InscriptionsOfAsoka.NewEditionByE.Hultzsch/HultzschCorpusAsokaSearchable_djvu.txt)
- RE XIII (Kalsi) section Q, with Antiyoga and four kings beyond him; Hultzsch's footnote identifies them as Ptolemy II, Antigonus Gonatas, Magas of Cyrene and Alexander (of Epirus or, "more probably", Corinth). OCR damages parts of the Kalsi list, so only the clean first clause is quoted.
- Kandahar: Sircar (1979) gives the Greek for RE XIII ("One hundred and fifty thousand persons were captured there and deported from there ...") and says the 1958 bilingual was Greek for Yavanas and Aramaic for Kambojas, "Iranians" — [Sircar, Asokan Studies](https://archive.org/stream/in.gov.ignca.67068/67068_djvu.txt). Wikipedia reproduces the Greek-half translation ("made known (the doctrine of) Piety"), the Aramaic ("became the institutor of Truth") and Wheeler's translation of the Greek plaque of 1963 — [bilingual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kandahar_Bilingual_Rock_Inscription), [Greek plaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kandahar_Greek_Edicts_of_Ashoka)

### Behistun
- DB I.6: "Drangiana , Aria , Chorasmia , Bactria , Sogdia , Gandara , Scythia , Sattagydia , Arachosia and Maka ; twenty-three lands in all" — [Livius T 02](https://www.livius.org/sources/content/behistun-persian-text/behistun-t-02/)
- DB IV.70: "it was in Aryan script" — [Livius T 42](https://www.livius.org/sources/content/behistun-persian-text/behistun-t-42/). Livius page header gives Darius r. 522-486 BCE.

## Sceptical and alternative pass
- Strongest caution recorded: Gentile (2019) notes "some scepticism (Kammenhuber 1968; Diakonov 1972)" about the Indo-Aryan reading of the Mitanni forms, and that "it is debated whether these language forms derive from Proto-Indo-Iranian or Proto-Indo-Aryan", so he uses "Aryan" neutrally after Thieme 1960. I did not open Kammenhuber, Diakonov or Thieme. Both Gentile and Burrow (1973) side with the Indo-Aryan view, so the page evidence is one-sided in that direction and the sceptics' own arguments are absent. Gentile also says van Koppen asks whether the Aryan names in Mitanni are original or secondary traits of the state.
- Dating caution: Rigveda dates are internal to the hymns (Jamison-Brereton via Wikipedia). Mills's Gathic range is derived from the Rigveda's, and Wikipedia says Witzel (2019) uses Mitanni loanwords to confirm a Rigveda date of c. 1200-1000 BCE, so the three dates support one another rather than being independent checks.
- Livius cautions that Vedic and Gathic "have a common ancestor, but developed differently" (ahura vs asura), so the match shows shared inheritance, not Indian-Iranian contact in the historical period.
- The Mitanni treaty names are attested in a Hittite-archive text; no source opened documents a route from India or from Iran into Mitanni (medium recorded as `unknown`, directness `inferred` or `debated`).
- Ashoka's claims about Greek kings are his own royal statements; I opened no independent Hellenistic source that records the missions. Those strands are marked `debated`; only the Kandahar finds, which are physical documents in Greek and Aramaic, are marked `documented`.
- Behistun and the Rigveda or Avesta are not linked by any text I opened. The Behistun lines give the eastern frontier (Gandara, Arachosia) and Darius's use of "Aryan" for his script, and nothing more.
