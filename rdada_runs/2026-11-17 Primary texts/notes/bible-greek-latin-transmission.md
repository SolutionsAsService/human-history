# bible-greek-latin-transmission: How the Hebrew Bible reached Greek and Latin readers

## Ask
Quote the primary evidence for how the Hebrew Bible reached Greek and Latin readers: the Letter of Aristeas, the Prologue of Ben Sira (Sirach), the Septuagint and its earliest manuscripts, the Great Isaiah Scroll as the oldest complete witness, Jerome's prologues. Separate composition, earliest witness and edition for each, with scholarly caution on the Letter of Aristeas's legendary details.

## Cited findings
Dates kept apart (composition / earliest witness / edition) in each candidate's `text` object.
- Aristeas: Demetrius of Phalerum, head of the king's library, wants the Jewish law translated; the translation takes 72 days — [Charles transl., CCEL](https://www.ccel.org/c/charles/otpseudepig/aristeas.htm). Composition c. 200-100 BCE (disputed); Greek manuscripts medieval.
- Sirach Prologue: grandson came to Egypt "in the eight and thirtieth year ... when Euergetes was king" and says the Law, Prophets and other books differ in Greek from the Hebrew — [KJV Apocrypha on Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_(King_James)/Wisdom_of_Sirach). Date c. 132-130 BCE; which Euergetes was not checked in an opened source.
- Philo adds identical wording by inspiration — [Yonge, Life of Moses II](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/yonge/book25.html).
- Jerome, Prologus Galeatus (c. 391): Sirach and other books outside the Hebrew canon; invites comparison with Greek and Latin manuscripts — [bible-researcher.com](https://www.bible-researcher.com/jerome.html).
- Jerome, Genesis preface (dated c. 406 by fourthcentury.com, which I opened; NPNF's Vulgate span 391-404): mocks the "seventy cells" story and says Ptolemy's Platonism explains Greek omissions — [Pearse transl.](https://www.tertullian.org/fathers/jerome_preface_genesis.htm), [fourthcentury.com](https://www.fourthcentury.com/jeromes-preface-to-the-pentateuch-c-a-d-406/).
- Dead Sea Scrolls: IAA says many Qumran biblical texts resemble the Masoretic Text — [deadseascrolls.org.il](https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/learn-about-the-scrolls/introduction). 1QIsa-a: >2600 variants against the Masoretic codex; Isa 53:11 agrees with the Septuagint — [Wikipedia Isaiah Scroll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaiah_Scroll) (secondary). MT Isa 53:11 in JPS: [Sefaria API](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Isaiah.53.11?context=0).
- Septuagint: oldest MSS 2nd c. BCE fragments of Leviticus and Deuteronomy — [Wikipedia Septuagint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Septuagint) (secondary).

## Sceptical and alternative pass
- [Jewish Encyclopedia, "Aristeas, Letter of"](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/1765-aristeas-letter-of): author a Jew not a courtier; historical statements about Philadelphus unreliable; Demetrius was banished at Philadelphus's accession; dating ranges from c. 200 BCE (Schürer) to Maccabean times; the Sirach prologue argues against an early date; the approvals are legitimation of the version.
- Jerome himself rejects the cells story; Brenton (1844, via Wikipedia) calls it pious fiction.
- Sceptical to the Isaiah scroll: variants count shows it is not simply the Masoretic text. Support is mostly one-sided for the Hebrew-to-Greek fact (Sirach prologue) and sceptical on Aristeas's details.
