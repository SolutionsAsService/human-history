# transmission-to-modern-chemistry: Transmission to modern chemistry (round 2)

## Ask
How did Greco-Egyptian alchemy reach modern chemistry (Byzantine collections, Arabic translation, Jabirian/Rhazes, Latin translations such as Robert of Chester 1144, Boyle, Newton, Lavoisier), which links are documented, what do Newman and Principe say about alchemy vs chemistry, and what do sceptics say about "Egyptian science" and pharaonic-era Hermes/Emerald Tablet claims?

## Cited findings
- Newman and Principe challenge a sharp alchemy/chemistry distinction via Boyle and Starkey; Boyle himself presented chemistry as separate from alchemical roots — [O'Connor review, PMC539611](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC539611)
- "Chymistry" is used deliberately because clear distinctions are held anachronistic; the volume spans medieval alchemy to mid-18th-c. chymistry — [Roos review, PMC2570458](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2570458)
- Newman: alchemy contributed to Boyle's mechanical philosophy; names medieval Geber among predecessors (publisher blurb) — [UCP](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/A/bo3750417.html)
- Principe's own chain: Greco-Egyptian chemeia, Arabic al-Kimiya, Medieval Latin alchemia, early modern chymistry (table of contents) — [UCP](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/S/bo12335123.html)
- Jabirian corpus (500+ titles) doubted as one author's work; cites Kraus 1943 and Haq 1994 — [Amr & Tbakhi, PMC6077026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6077026)
- Robert of Chester's Latin translation of the Morienus dialogue, 11 Feb 1144, generally the first Latin alchemy translation — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liber_de_compositione_alchemiae)
- Rhazes' operations overlap with pre-Islamic Alexandrian ones — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alchemy_in_the_medieval_Islamic_world)
- Emerald Tablet's earliest source is Arabic, c. 8th-9th c., partly from Greek/Syriac material — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerald_Tablet)
- Hermetica c. 100-300 CE, Greek-Egyptian products (Fowden); Bull (2018) argues Egyptian priests — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermetica)
- Newton's occult studies are largely alchemy — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Newton%27s_occult_studies)

## Sceptical and alternative pass
Opened: Stolen Legacy (Wikipedia), where historians call James's Egypt-origin thesis pseudohistory; this is an analogue, not a chemistry-specific rebuttal. The Emerald Tablet and Hermetica pages give the dating evidence against pharaonic origin, but Bull's 2018 view (Egyptian priests as authors) is the alternative reading and keeps the Egypt link alive for the Roman era. Search snippets (not opened) suggested Călian and Roos question Principe and Newman's reading of Decknamen as chemical rather than spiritual, and that Gillispie denied Newton practised alchemy; not usable as candidates. No scholarly source specifically rebutting "modern chemistry is Egyptian science" was found or opened.
