# khem-name-chemistry-etymology: Origin of 'chemistry'/'alchemy' and Kemet

## Ask
1. What is the evidence and scholarly status of the claim that 'chemistry'/'alchemy' derives from Kemet/Khem/'black land' (kmt)? Competing etymologies, who argues which, and which is most widely accepted?
2. What do the earliest attestations of chemeia/chymeia say (Zosimus, Suda, Plutarch's Chemia)?

## Cited findings
- Two main views are presented: Greek chēmeia ('cast together', from khuma/khéō, attributed to Mahn) and Egyptian kmt > Demotic kmỉ > Coptic kēme (Budge). Mahn is reported as calling the Egyptian theory possibly folk etymology. — [Etymology of chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology_of_chemistry)
- Diels (1914) derived the word from Greek χύμα (cast metal). Zosimus derived it from a book, the Khemeu. Arabic al-kīmiyā borrowed Late Greek khēmeía. — [Alchemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alchemy)
- Zosimus (fl. c. AD 300) via Syncellus links the art to a book 'chema'. — [Zosimos of Panopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zosimos_of_Panopolis)
- Egypt's name km.t 'black' (Nile soil vs red desert), Coptic Kēmə, Early Greek Khēmía exist independently of any alchemy link. — [Names of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Egypt)
- Diocletian burning of Egyptian gold/silver books (Gibbon: 'first authentic event in the history of alchymy'). — [Diocletian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocletian)
- Accepted status (my reading of what was opened, not a scholarly consensus statement): no source opened declares a winner; Arabic al-kīmiyā from Late Greek khēmeía is uncontested, and the Egyptian vs Greek (chuma) origin of the Greek word remains disputed. Newman & Principe 1998 and OED are cited by Wikipedia but were not opened.
