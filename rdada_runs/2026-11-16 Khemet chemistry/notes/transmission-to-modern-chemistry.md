# transmission-to-modern-chemistry: Transmission to modern chemistry

## Ask
How did Greco-Egyptian alchemy reach modern chemistry (Byzantine collections, Arabic translation, Jabirian/Rhazes work, Latin translations such as Robert of Chester 1144, European alchemy, Boyle, Newton, Lavoisier), and which links are documented in texts and manuscripts? What does the historiography (Newman, Principe) say about alchemy vs chemistry, and what do sceptics say about "Egyptian science" and pharaonic-era Hermes/Emerald Tablet claims?

## Cited findings
All from Wikipedia (tertiary), the only host reachable. Treat as pointers, not evidence.
- Emerald Tablet earliest text is Arabic, compiled late 8th/early 9th c.; proposed 750-833 CE — [Emerald Tablet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerald_Tablet)
- Jabirian corpus: Kraus c. 850-950 dating, widely accepted; Haq (1994) criticises; pseudonymous — [Jabir ibn Hayyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabir_ibn_Hayyan)
- Robert of Chester, Liber de compositione alchemiae, 1144, first Latin alchemy book — [Robert of Chester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_of_Chester)
- Corpus Hermeticum c. 100-300 CE, Koine Greek, citing Copenhaver 1992 and Bull 2018 — [Corpus Hermeticum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpus_Hermeticum)
- chemia/alchemia synonyms in early modern period; Zosimos fl. c. 300; Byzantine and Arabic transmission; pharaonic origin has "little to no evidence" — [Alchemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alchemy)
