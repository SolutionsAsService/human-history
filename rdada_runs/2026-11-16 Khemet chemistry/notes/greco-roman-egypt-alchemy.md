# greco-roman-egypt-alchemy: Alchemy and early chemistry in Greco-Roman Egypt

## Ask
What textual and material evidence shows alchemy / early chemistry in Greco-Roman Egypt (Alexandria, Panopolis): Leiden papyrus X, Stockholm papyrus, Pseudo-Democritus Physika kai Mystika, Zosimus of Panopolis, Mary the Jewess (Maria Prophetissa) and her apparatus, Cleopatra the Alchemist? Dates, languages, and what they actually describe (practical dye/metal recipes versus theory).

Is there any evidence for alchemy in pharaonic Egypt before the Ptolemies? State what is absent as well as what is present.

## Cited findings
- Leyden papyrus X: Greek codex, c. end of 3rd century CE, 111 recipes (metals, imitation gold and silver, stones, purple dye, inks); "exclusively practical", no alchemical or philosophical elements. [Source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leyden_papyrus_X)
- Stockholm papyrus: Greek recipe collection c. 300 CE, 154 recipes (dyeing, gems, pearls, imitation gold/silver); Leyden X shares sources, probably hand and ink; some recipes may derive from the Pseudo-Democritus corpus. [Source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_papyrus)
- Zosimos of Panopolis: c. 300 CE, Greek (parts in Syriac/Arabic); mixes apparatus with visionary/Hermetic allegory. [Source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zosimos_of_Panopolis)
- Mary the Jewess: 1st to 3rd century CE, known through Zosimos quotations; tribikos/kerotakis/bain-marie attribution disputed. [Source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_the_Jewess)
- Cleopatra the Alchemist: pseudonym, 3rd to 4th century CE; Chrysopoeia survives in 10th to 11th century copies. [Source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleopatra_the_Alchemist)
- Pseudo-Democritus Four Books: probably 1st century CE; Zosimos claimed a pharaonic priestly origin, "little to no evidence". [Source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alchemy)
