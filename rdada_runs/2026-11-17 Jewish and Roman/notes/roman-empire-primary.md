# roman-empire-primary: Roman primary sources on the empire

## Ask
Quote Roman primary sources on the empire itself: Res Gestae Divi Augusti (Ankara inscription; Greek and Latin text), Polybius 6.11-18, Caesar Gallic War 1.1 and 6.11-28, Livy Preface, Tacitus Agricola 21, the Constitutio Antoniniana (P.Giss. 40 and Cassius Dio 78.9), Ammianus 31.2. Composition dates with basis, earliest witness, and scholarly cautions (the Dio reason for 212, Caesar's ethnography as propaganda).

## Cited findings
All 23 quotes were confirmed with check_quote.py (exit 0). 14 are primary passages (`text` objects), 9 scholarship.
- Res Gestae heading, Latin (Latin Library) and Greek (LacusCurtius Loeb): the work claims to record deeds "by which he placed the whole world under the sovereignty of the Roman people" — [Latin](https://www.thelatinlibrary.com/resgestae.html), [Greek/English](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Augustus/Res_Gestae/1*.html)

### Dates (kept separate)
- Res Gestae: composition begun well before 14 CE, last additions 14 CE (Shipley, Loeb intro). Witness: Ancyra copy inscribed after Augustus's death, "perhaps as late as 19 CE" (Heurist summarising Krencker and Schede); bronze originals lost. Edition: Loeb Shipley 1924 on Mommsen 1883.
- Polybius 6: disputed, Mioni and Ziegler before 146 BCE, Erbse well after (Brink and Walbank, CQ 1954). Witness not established.
- Caesar BG: 57-54 BCE start per the CAMWS abstract; Nipperdey (all 50 BCE), Adcock, Wiseman as summarised on Wikipedia (opened). Earliest MSS named there: 9th century.
- Livy pr.: 27-25 BCE older view vs Burton 33/32 BCE (CAMWS abstract). Tacitus Agricola: 98 CE (Audano, in Italian). Ammianus: 380s, before 391 (Wikipedia only, weakest). Dio: Wikipedia quoting Dio, 22 years of work, covers to 229 (weak).
- P.Giss. 40: edict dated 212 (inscriptions with new Aurelii by 212/3); papyrus 211-215 CE (Heurist), early third century (ostia-antica.org).

## Sceptical and alternative pass
- Dio's reason for 212: the Heurist commentary says the edict's own stated motive is absent from the sources and Dio's fiscal reason is the only note; the papyrus is fragmentary, line 9's exception "dediticii" is a restoration, and the extent of "universal" is uncertain. Separately candidate-ised.
- Caesar's ethnography as propaganda: Allen-Hornblower (CQ 2014) and Creer's CAMWS abstract (candidates, stance fails_to_support). Livius (opened) calls the Res Gestae "propaganda" ("In other words - it is propaganda"), not made a candidate.
- Ammianus Huns: Burgersdijk (2016) calls the account largely fictitious and topos-driven; CAMWS Ammianus/Priscus abstract cites King 1995 (not opened). Woudhuysen's dissent seen only as a search snippet.
- Romanization (Agricola 21): searched for Mattingly/Woolf, nothing opened. Only Audano's dating and a DCC commentary note were opened; the sceptical literature is a gap.
- The scholarship side is thin on the Res Gestae (Shipley 1924 and a database summary; Cooley's commentary seen as front matter only).
