# roman-histories-on-jews-christians: Roman and Greek historians on Jews and Christians (primary texts)

## Ask
- Quote Roman and Greek historians on Jews and Christians from open editions: Tacitus Annals 15.44 and Histories 5.1-13, Suetonius Claudius 25.4 and Nero 16, Cassius Dio 66.4-7 and 69.12-14, Pliny the Elder Natural History 5.73, Philo's Embassy to Gaius and Against Flaccus.
- Composition dates with basis, earliest manuscript witness (the Medicean for Tacitus Annals 15), and scholarly cautions (authenticity of the Tacitus passage, Chrestus identification).

All 22 quotes in the candidates file were confirmed with `check_quote.py` against the cited URL on 2026-11-17 (the first run failed twice, one on a footnote marker inside the Suetonius quote and one on a transient Wikipedia fetch; both were fixed or re-run and now pass).

## Sceptical and alternative pass
- **Authenticity of Annals 15.44.** Searched for interpolation arguments and for Brent Shaw's 2015 JRS article. Shaw's abstract (opened, full text paywalled) says the passage is "probably genuine Tacitus" and attacks the persecution's historicity and the term Chrestiani as anachronism, not authorship. C. P. Jones' 2017 reply (opened, Cambridge Core HTML) defends the report. The Wikipedia 'Tacitus on Jesus' page (opened) says most scholars hold the passage authentic and notes Yelnitsky's interpolation argument from "procurator". The real live dispute is about historicity and about the sole-manuscript text, not a Christian interpolation. Of the cautions, this is the weakest-sourced for the interpolation side: I found no first-hand interpolation argument I could open.
- **Manuscript reading.** Jones: the Second Medicean first read Chrestiani and a later hand changed it (candidate 17). I did not see the folio.
- **Chrestus.** Searched for the identification debate. The Wikipedia 'Claudius' expulsion' and 'Suetonius on Christians' pages (opened) record the split: Evans, Meier, Keener and Stern for Jesus; Benko (unknown agitator), Slingerland and Elliott against. Dio 60.6.6 (opened) says Claudius did not expel the Jews but banned meetings, which complicates reading Suetonius as one clear expulsion. The Judaism and Rome project commentary (opened) lists Acts 18:2, Suetonius, Dio and Orosius together. I did not open the named scholars' own works.
- **Dio on Bar Kokhba.** Dio 69.12-14 never names Bar Kokhba; the revolt link is from other sources. Dio's causes and casualty figures are his own.
- **One-sidedness.** The primary texts are Roman, hostile or indifferent; the two Philo texts are Jewish and partisan. No Jewish or Christian counter-text (Josephus, Acts, Orosius) was fetched, because the brief asked for Roman and Greek historians.
