# dead-sea-scrolls: The Dead Sea Scrolls (primary texts, dating, authorship)

Researched 2026-11-17. 23 candidates in `dead-sea-scrolls.candidates.json`. Every quote was confirmed with `check_quote.py` against the cited page (exception for the Hebrew Masoretic line, see Gaps).

## Ask
Read and quote the Dead Sea Scrolls themselves from open translations or scholarly pages: the Community Rule (1QS, the two spirits and the Teacher), the War Scroll (1QM), the Damascus Document, Pesher Habakkuk (1QpHab), the Temple Scroll, the Copper Scroll, the Great Isaiah Scroll and Cave 4 biblical manuscripts as witnesses to the Hebrew Bible. Give the discovery (1946-47, Cave 1) and the dating evidence: radiocarbon (Jull et al. 1995, Radiocarbon 37; Bonani 1992), palaeography, and the competing readings of who wrote them (Essene hypothesis vs Golb's Jerusalem-library theory vs others). Quote at loci with composition date, earliest witness and basis. Quote the strongest sceptical view.

## Cited findings

Primary passages
- 1QS III 17-21 (Vermes): God "appointed for him two spirits"; the children of righteousness are ruled by the Prince of Light, the children of falsehood by the Angel of Darkness. 1QS col. XI hand c. 100-75 BCE (Cross). — [CCAT 1QS](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/gopher/other/courses/rels/225/Texts/1QS); palaeography in [Jull et al. 1995](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/8ABC8DDCB3043F747D7701B19B5BA520/S0033822200014740a.pdf/radiocarbon-dating-of-scrolls-and-linen-fragments-from-the-judean-desert.pdf), App. 1
- CD I: God "raised for them a teacher of righteousness to make them walk in the way of His heart" (Schechter 1910, Genizah MSS). The Cave 4 copy 4Q266 is in a Hasmonean hand, 100-50 BCE, and was radiocarbon-dated. — [CCAT CD](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/gopher/other/courses/rels/225/Texts/CD)
- 1QpHab col. VII 4-5 (Teacher of Righteousness, to whom God made known the mysteries of the prophets) and col. VIII 8-13 (Wicked Priest). Copy early Herodian, c. 30-1 BCE; radiocarbon 2054 +/- 22 BP (DSS-3). — [CCAT Pesher Habakkuk](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/gopher/other/courses/rels/225/Texts/Pesher%20Habakkuk)
- 1QM 1:1-7 (Wise/Abegg/Cook): the Sons of Light attack "the Sons of Darkness, the army of Belial", with the Kittim and covenant-breakers. No date found on any page opened. — [PBS Frontline](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/pages/frontline/shows/religion/portrait/scrolltranslation.html)
- 4Q169 Pesher Nahum (Allegro): the Lion of Wrath "hangs men up alive" and the "man hanged alive upon a tree"; names Demetrius of Greece. — [CCAT Pesher Nahum](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/gopher/other/courses/rels/225/Texts/Pesher%20Nahum%20(part))
- 11Q19 Temple Scroll 64:6-13: informers and defectors to be hanged alive on the tree and buried the same day (Elgvin's translation, composition put in the 2nd c. BCE by Elgvin only; Wikipedia says no consensus). — [Elgvin, Themelios](https://thegospelcoalition.org/themelios/article/the-messiah-who-was-cursed-on-the-tree)
- 3Q15 Copper Scroll I: treasure "in the fortress which is in the Vale of Achor"; found 1952 in Cave 3, cut into 23 strips. No date in the source. — [USC WSRP](https://dornsife.usc.edu/wsrp/copper-scroll/)
- 4QMMT: "These are some of our regulations" (purity and Temple rulings), with Schiffman's Sadducean reading rebutted on Virtual Qumran. — [CCAT 4QMMT](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/gopher/other/courses/rels/225/Texts/4QMMT(new))
- Isa 53:11: 1QIsaa reads "He will see light and be satisfied"; 1QIsab, 4QIsad and the LXX agree; the Masoretic Text has no "light". 1QIsaa hand c. 150-125 BCE (Cross; Avigad, second half 2nd c. BCE); radiocarbon 2136 +/- 24 BP weighted mean (Zurich + Arizona). — [Meade, ETC blog](https://evangelicaltextualcriticism.blogspot.com/2020/04/he-will-see-light-in-isaiah-5311.html); MT via [Sefaria API](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Isaiah_53.11?context=0&commentary=0)

Discovery
- Great Isaiah Scroll "first discovered by Bedouin shepherds in 1947 from Qumran Cave 1"; sold via Kando to Mar Samuel; photographed by ASOR in 1948. Context only (encyclopedia). — [Wikipedia, Isaiah Scroll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaiah_Scroll)

Radiocarbon and palaeography
- Jull et al. 1995 (Arizona AMS, 18 texts + 2 linens): "in good agreement with estimates of age based on paleography"; one exception (first 4Q258 sample). Samples taken March 1994 at Rockefeller and Israel Museums; control papyri of known age run blind. Two documentary texts (4Q342, 4Q344) can be as late as Bar-Kokhba and were probably mixed in by dealers (Broshi). The dates are for the parchment, not composition. DOI 10.1017/S0033822200014740.
- Bonani et al. 1992 (Zurich AMS, 14 scrolls): calibrated ages "agree well, except in one case" with palaeography or stated dates. Abstract page only. DOI 10.1017/S0033822200064158. — [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/radiocarbon-dating-of-fourteen-dead-sea-scrolls/3D21205A1B209878952A8EBB31771077)
- Rodley 1993: conventional ages "offset systematically by about +40 yr"; abstract only. — [Cambridge](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/an-assessment-of-the-radiocarbon-dating-of-the-dead-sea-scrolls/21C881C07D5F03597FE3E69FD71C6A33)
- Rodley and Thiering 1999 (Radiocarbon 41): re-read Bonani, Jull and Doudna's analysis and argue the Teacher and Wicked Priest may be 1st c. CE, "contemporary with the founders of Christianity"; they note 14C dates the writing material, not composition, and reject Doudna's "the first century CE disappears from Qumran". — [Cambridge PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/B643B2523828D99A5E451A94D4041728/S0033822200019524a.pdf/use-of-radiocarbon-dating-in-assessing-christian-connections-to-the-dead-sea-scrolls.pdf)

Who wrote them
- Essene hypothesis: Josephus War 2.119 (three sects) and Pliny NH 5.73 (Essenes on the west shore of the Dead Sea, above Engedi). Virtual Qumran (Hebrew University): the "Essene hypothesis" remains the best way to interpret the evidence, but with a flexible label. — [Josephus, CCAT](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/gopher/other/courses/rels/225/Texts/Josephus%20War2); [Pliny, Harland](https://www.philipharland.com/Blog/2022/07/judeans-pliny-the-elder-and-julius-solinus-on-the-essenes-beside-the-dead-sea-first-century-ce/); [Virtual Qumran](https://virtualqumran.huji.ac.il/scrolls/SectQumran.htm)
- Golb (Jerusalem libraries): the Scrolls were "Taken from Jerusalem libraries and personal collections ... and hidden away in many places in the wilderness"; over fifty hands in Cave 1; the editors treated the Qumran-Essene hypothesis as a fact. — [Golb, Fathom](https://fathom.lib.uchicago.edu/1/777777190227/)
- Sadducean or Zadokite: argued from 4QMMT and the 364-day calendar; Virtual Qumran says it "does not seem justified by the evidence". — [Virtual Qumran](https://virtualqumran.huji.ac.il/moreInfo/SadduceesEssenes.htm)
- Others: Boccaccini (Enochic-Essene parent movement, Qumran a radicalised offshoot); Collins (a varied yahad; Qumran "at most ... one settlement of the yahad"; Teacher in the first half of the 1st c. BCE); Magness's later dating of the Hellenistic occupation to the first half of the 1st c. BCE. — [ASOR blog](https://asorblog.org/2013/12/13/rethinking-the-qumran-community-recent-approaches.html)

Transmission strands
- Iranian influence on the two-spirits dualism: argued by Dupont-Sommer and Kuhn and in the Iranica entry (inferred, no route). Hempel: the full corpus shows "an erosion of the prominence of the dualistic ideas". — [Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/dead-sea-scrolls); [Hempel abstract](https://orion-bibliography.huji.ac.il/node/59688)

## Sceptical and alternative pass
- Radiocarbon: sceptical or alternative readings are Rodley 1993 (ages about 40 yr too old, which tightens agreement rather than breaking it) and Rodley and Thiering 1999 (the CE-date alternative). Both authors lean toward a later setting than the mainstream; Thiering's reading of the pesharim is a minority position. I could not open Doudna 1998 (see Gaps), so the strongest published radiocarbon critique is reported only second-hand through Rodley and Thiering.
- Authorship: Golb is the strongest dissent and is quoted in his own words. The counter-case comes from Virtual Qumran and Collins/Boccaccini. The Essene-versus-Ein-Gedi dispute over Pliny's "below" was seen in search snippets only and is not claimed.
- Dualism: Iranian-influence claim (Duchesne-Guillemin) is paired with Hempel's sceptical abstract.
- Temple Scroll: the only open date I found is Elgvin's; Wikipedia says there is no consensus on date, origin or Qumran link, and 11Q19 itself was not seen.
- A one-sided spot: the Great Isaiah and Cave 4 biblical manuscripts are represented by one textual-critical blog and one encyclopedia page; no peer-reviewed text-critical source was opened, so the "witness to the Hebrew Bible" case is thin.
