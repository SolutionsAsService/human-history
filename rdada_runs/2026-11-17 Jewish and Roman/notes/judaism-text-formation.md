# judaism-text-formation: How the Hebrew Bible and rabbinic texts took shape (primary texts)

## Ask
1. "Quote the sources for how the Hebrew Bible and rabbinic texts took shape: Josephus Against Apion 1.38-41 (twenty-two books), 4 Ezra 14 on the ninety-four books, Mishnah Yadayim 3:5 and Bava Batra 14b (canon and authorship lists; use Sefaria via fetch_text.py), the Masoretic colophons (Aleppo Codex, Leningrad Codex B19a), and the Mishnah's own account of its transmission (Avot 1:1)."
2. "Composition dates with basis, earliest witness, and what scholars dispute (the 'Council of Jamnia' as a myth, when the canon closed)."

All 19 candidate quotes were confirmed with `check_quote.py` against the cited URL (Bava Batra 14b/15a: against the Sefaria text as printed by `fetch_text.py`, because the API JSON carries bold markup that splits phrases). Dates in `text.composition` come from pages I opened (named in each `basis`), not memory. Composition, witness and edition are kept separate in each `text` object.

## Cited findings
- Josephus says the Jews have "only twenty-two books... justly believed to be divine": five of Moses, thirteen prophetic books to Artaxerxes, four of hymns and precepts; later history lacks the same authority because "there has not been an exact succession of prophets" — [Lexundria (Whiston)](https://lexundria.com/j_ap/1.37-1.43/wst), [CoJS](https://cojs.org/?p=6420). Against Apion cites Antiquities, so is after 94 CE; Lexundria headers it c. AD 97 — [Wikipedia, Against Apion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Against_Apion).
- 4 Ezra 14: the law is "burned" (14:21); Ezra dictates ninety-four books in forty days (14:44); twenty-four to be public, seventy kept for the wise (14:45-46) — [2 Esdras 14, RSV](https://biblia.com/bible/rsv/2Esd14). The work was composed in the last decade of the 1st c. CE (Flavian emperors in the Eagle Vision), earliest surviving quotation Clement of Alexandria, late 2nd c. — [Fortress introduction](https://ms.fortresspress.com/downloads/9780800699680Intro.pdf).
- Mishnah Yadayim 3:5: "All the Holy Scriptures defile the hands. The Song of Songs and Kohelet... defile the hands"; dispute over Kohelet/Song of Songs; Ben Azzai's tradition ties a ruling to the day Eleazar ben Azariah was appointed head of the academy; Akiva says "If they had a dispute... only about Kohelet" — [Sefaria, Kulp](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Mishnah_Yadayim.3.5?context=0&commentary=0), [CoJS, Schiffman/Berrin](https://cojs.org/Mishnah_Yadayim_3-5-_The_Debate_Over_the_Biblical_Canon/). Tosefta Yadayim 2:14 argues Song of Songs is inspired, Ecclesiastes "only the wisdom of Solomon" — [CoJS](https://cojs.org/tosefta_yadayim_2-14-_the_biblical_canon_and_divine_inspiration/).
- Bava Batra 14b-15a: baraita on the order of Prophets and Writings (Ruth, Psalms, Job, Proverbs; Ecclesiastes, Song of Songs, Lamentations; Daniel, Esther; Ezra, Chronicles) and on authorship (Moses wrote Torah, Balaam, Job; Joshua his book plus eight Torah verses; Samuel, Judges, Ruth; David Psalms via ten elders; Jeremiah, Kings, Lamentations; Hezekiah's circle Isaiah, Proverbs, Song, Ecclesiastes; the Great Assembly Ezekiel, the Twelve, Daniel, Esther; Ezra his book and Chronicles) — [Sefaria 14b](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Bava_Batra.14b?context=0&commentary=0), [15a](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Bava_Batra.15a?context=0&commentary=0).
- Avot 1:1: Sinai to Joshua, elders, prophets, Men of the Great Assembly — [Sefaria, Kulp](https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Pirkei_Avot.1.1?context=0&commentary=0).
- Mishnah redaction ca. 200 CE by Judah the Prince, as the end of a process — [CoJS/Schiffman](https://cojs.org/the_redaction_of_the_mishnah/). Earliest extant material witness to rabbinic literature of any kind is 6th-7th c. (Mosaic of Rehob); Mishnah codices Parma A (late 11th c.), Kaufmann (12th-13th c.) — [Wikipedia, Mishnah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mishnah). The Babylonian Talmud is compiled 3rd-6th c.; earliest Genizah fragments late 7th/8th c.; only complete manuscript Munich 95 (1342) — [Wikipedia, Talmud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talmud).
- Aleppo Codex colophon (as rendered by Haberman): written by Shlomo ben Boya'a, vocalized and transmitted by Aaron ben Asher, 929 CE — [Hakirah](https://hakirah.org/Vol21Haberman.pdf); Wikipedia says copied c. 920 and given to the Karaites 1040-1050, Haberman c. 1064 — [Wikipedia, Aleppo Codex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleppo_Codex).
- Leningrad Codex colophon: Samuel ben Jacob wrote, pointed and added Masorah from books corrected by Aaron ben Moses ben Asher — [Wikiquote](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Leningrad_Codex); dated AD 1008 (or 1009) by its colophon — [Wikipedia, Leningrad Codex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad_Codex).
- The Dead Sea Scrolls (c. 150 BCE - 75 CE) show no uniform Hebrew text — [Wikipedia, Masoretic Text](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masoretic_Text).

## Sceptical and alternative pass
- **"Council of Jamnia".** Christie (JTS 1925) is the earliest dissent: Ben Azzai's report does not show a canon decision that day, debates on Esther, Canticles and Koheleth continued to c. AD 200, and "there never seems to have been a formal canonizing" ([JTS pdf](https://biblicalstudies.org.uk/pdf/jts/026_347.pdf)). Wikipedia (tertiary) summarises Graetz 1871, Lewis 1964, Leiman 1976, Sundberg 1997 ("No book is ever mentioned in the sources as being excluded from the canon at Jabneh"), and that the date of closure is still debated, with Davies favouring the Hasmoneans ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Jamnia)). I could not open Lewis, Leiman or Aune directly.
- **When the canon closed.** Gallagher's review of Lim 2013: no closed canon asserted before the third century CE; Qumran does not say what its Bible was ([Marginalia](https://themarginaliareview.com/?p=4678)). The same reviewer notes camps from "Hasmonean closure" to "late and gradual". Josephus's 22 and 4 Ezra's 24 are the earliest numbers, both late 1st c. CE, both after the Temple's fall; neither names the books. Mishnah Yadayim records a dispute over two books; it does not list a canon. Bava Batra gives an order and authorship, not closure.
- **Alternative reading** (a council or formal closure at Yavneh): found only as the target of the critiques above and, in a search summary, Britannica ("a rabbinic council around 100 CE seems to have ruled"); Britannica was Cloudflare-blocked, so not opened and not recorded.
- Evidence is not one-sided on dating: the Wikipedia 2 Esdras date (90-100) and the Fortress date (last decade of 1st c.) agree; Aleppo dating differs (920 vs 929).
