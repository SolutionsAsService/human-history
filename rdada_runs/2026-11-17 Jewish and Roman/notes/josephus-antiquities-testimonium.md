## Ask

Transmission:
- Eusebius HE 1.11.7-8 quotes the Testimonium right after the John passage, in essentially the extant wording — [NPNF2 vol.1 (New Advent)](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250101.htm). Terminus ante quem 324 per [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephus_on_Jesus), citing Louth.
- Eusebius HE 2.23.20-22 ascribes to Josephus a sentence about James's death avenging the fall of Jerusalem (not in extant text) and then quotes Ant. 20 — [New Advent](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250102.htm)
- Origen, Contra Celsum 1.47: Josephus, "not believing in Jesus as the Christ", on John and on James; Origen does not quote the Testimonium there — [ANF 4 (New Advent)](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/04161.htm)
- Jerome, De viris illustribus 13: Latin variant "was believed to be Christ" — [NPNF2 vol.3 (New Advent)](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/2708.htm)
- Agapius (Kitab al-'Unwan, 942 CE): Arabic Testimonium, "perhaps the Messiah", Pines's translation as printed in [Hillar 2004](https://www.socinian.org/files/Testimonium_Flavianum_FULL_TEXT.pdf). Hillar also says Agapius's text is probably translated from Syriac and that Michael the Syrian (1195) has a Syriac version.
- Earliest Greek MS with the Testimonium: Ambrosianus 370 (F 128), 11th c.; about 120 Greek and 170 Latin witnesses, Latin from the 6th c. — [Wikipedia, Josephus on Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephus_on_Jesus) (citing Mason 2001). Secondary only.

Composition (kept apart from witness and edition):
- Composition 93-94 CE: Josephus's own date at 20.12.1 (above); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiquities_of_the_Jews) puts it in Domitian's 13th year (infobox 93/94 CE, text 94); [Kirby](https://earlychristianwritings.com/testimonium.html) says "around 93 CE". No professional-academic dating was open (see Gaps), so the 93-94 range rests on the author's own date plus these summaries.
- Witnesses: Eusebius (by 324 CE) quoting; Greek MSS 11th c.; Latin 6th c.; Syriac/Arabic 10th-12th c. Edition quoted: Whiston 1737 (Perseus, 1895 printing).

Scholarly positions:
- Spread of opinion, 1937-1980 (Feldman's tally as reported by Kirby): 4 entirely genuine, 6 mostly genuine, 20 genuine with some interpolations, 9 with several, 13 totally interpolated — [Kirby](https://earlychristianwritings.com/testimonium.html).
- Partial authenticity: Meier (core plus three Christian interpolations; pre-Eusebian silence explained because the core did not support Jesus's divinity) via [Kirby](https://earlychristianwritings.com/testimonium.html); Ehrman ("The majority of scholars of early Judaism, and experts on Josephus, think that ... one or more Christian scribes 'touched up' the passage a bit", p. 60, as quoted on [Vridar](https://vridar.org/2012/04/27/6-earl-dohertys-response-to-bart-ehrmans-case-against-mythicism-jewish-sources/)). Wikipedia (citing Maier) says a large majority of modern scholars accept partial authenticity.
- Full forgery, Eusebian authorship: Olson first-hand on [The Jesus Blog](https://historicaljesusresearch.blogspot.com/2013/08/the-testimonium-flavianum-eusebius-and.html) (also reports Feldman 2012 leaning that way: "may well have interpolated such a statement"); Carrier first-hand in [Jesus among the Historians](https://richardcarrier.info/testimonium.pdf) (deliberate addition by Eusebius or Pamphilus; James phrase an accidental interpolation; Arabic version unreliable).
- Arabic and Syriac: Pines (1971) brought Agapius and Michael to light; Whealey (2008) argues both derive from the Syriac Eusebius, Michael nearer to Josephus — summarised on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephus_on_Jesus).

## Sceptical and alternative pass
Done. Sceptical sources: Olson, Carrier, Whealey (via Wikipedia, against Agapius's independence). Authenticity-leaning sources: Meier, Ehrman, Feldman's older survey, Wikipedia's citation of Maier. The one-sidedness to note is on the open scholarship, not the primary texts: first-hand voices that were opened are the sceptics (Olson, Carrier); the authenticity side (Meier, Ehrman, Whealey, Pines, Feldman) is only second-hand, because those books were not open. Treat Hillar's and Kirby's pages as mediating sources with their own stances (Kirby says the passage "cannot be entirely genuine"; Vridar is hosted by a mythicist-leaning blog arguing against Ehrman; Carrier is a mythicist author; his survey is self-published).

Two points visible in the texts themselves (not from scholarship): Whiston brackets "the" in "He was [the] Christ"; and the extant 20.9.1 has no sentence tying Jerusalem's fall to James, although Origen and Eusebius both ascribe one to Josephus.
