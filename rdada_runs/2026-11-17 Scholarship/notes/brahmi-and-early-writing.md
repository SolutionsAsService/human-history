# brahmi-and-early-writing: Brahmi and early writing (thread)

## Ask
Open modern scholarship on the origin of Brahmi (Salomon 1998; Falk 2018; Indi@logs hybrid-origin paper), the Wadi el-Hol inscriptions (Darnell et al. 2005), the Proto-Sinaitic decipherment debate, and the dating of Chinese and Mesoamerican writing (Cascajal block). State which proposals are widely accepted and which are minority views.

## Cited findings
- Brahmi origin: there is no consensus; Aramaic is the leading candidate but no letter-by-letter derivation is agreed; Pillai (2023) proposes a minority hybrid (Aramaic + Phoenician + Greek) invention — [Indi@logs](https://ddd.uab.cat/pub/indialogs/indialogs_a2023v10/indialogs_a2023v10p93.pdf)
- Baums: Kharosthi is Achaemenid-linked and no later than 4th c. BCE; no conclusive evidence of writing in mainland India before Asoka; invention of Brahmi on the Kharosthi model by Asoka or predecessors is "possible, though by no means certain" (minority/cautious view) — [Baums](https://stefanbaums.com/publications/baums_2016_1.pdf)
- Wadi el-Hol (Darnell et al. 2005, paywalled; read only via reviews): two inscriptions, at most 28 signs, undeciphered; dating (Middle Kingdom vs c.1300 BC) controversial — [Haring 2019](https://hdl.handle.net/1887/3295773), [Haring 2025](https://scholarlypublications.universiteitleiden.nl/access/item%3A4210941/view)
- Proto-Sinaitic: widely accepted as derived from Egyptian signs with acrophony; decipherment and date remain disputed — [Simons 2011](https://rosetta.bham.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Simons_Proto-Sinaitic_Rosetta9.pdf), [Höflmayer et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2020.157)
- Cascajal: authenticity supported archaeometrically (2020) but doubts over context persist; original claim summarised by Skidmore — [Englehardt et al.](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0956536119000257), [Skidmore](https://www.mesoweb.org/reports/Cascajal.pdf)
- Mesoamerica: the La Venta cylinder-seal "writing" doubted as iconic — [Mesoweb](https://www.mesoweb.org/reports/SanBartoloWriting.html)
- China: Jiahu signs read as ritual symbols, not writing — [Antiquity abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/6DDB3E0583ACB6896B3DBCAC143FA846)
