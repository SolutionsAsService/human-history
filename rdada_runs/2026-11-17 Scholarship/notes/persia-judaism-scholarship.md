# persia-judaism-scholarship: Did Persian (Zoroastrian) ideas shape Judaism?

## Ask
"Open the scholarship on whether Persian (Zoroastrian) ideas shaped Judaism: Barr, 'The Question of Religious Influence: The Case of Zoroastrianism, Judaism, and Christianity' (JAAR 1985), Mary Boyce, Grabbe on the Persian period, Bremmer 2002, Mark S. Smith 2001, and any open-access review article." / "Quote the strongest proponent and the strongest sceptic first-hand, with their stated reasons and limits."

## Result in one paragraph
14 candidates, every quote confirmed with check_quote.py. Honest limit: none of the five named scholars except Smith (his own short summary) and Boyce (chapter summary page only) was reached first-hand in their main works. Bremmer, Boyce's claims, Zaehner, Russell and Gignoux are all SECOND-HAND and labelled so in each candidate. Barr and Grabbe were not reached at all. The one genuinely first-hand proponent text is Shaked's typescript; the strongest first-hand sceptic is Isbell (JBQ 2006), which is itself a "cursory survey". Direction of the evidence: no source documents a transfer; every strand is `debated` or `inferred`.

## Cited findings
- Shaked (Iranist) says Iranists tend to assume great Iranian influence while Judaists explain Judaism internally; he lists angels/demons, eschatology and dualism as the post-exilic changes pointing to Persia, plus Persian loanwords, but adds the Jewish and Iranian dualisms "are not really the same system" — [Shaked typescript, AJA MS-603](https://collections.americanjewisharchives.org/ms/ms0603/ms0603.053.010.pdf)
- Isbell: no Persian loanword in the Torah; item-by-item rebuttal (dualism, Satan, angels, resurrection); Zoroastrian sources too late; quotes Shaked on the "stumbling block of the problem of chronology" — [Isbell, JBQ 34(3) 2006](https://jbq.jewishbible.org/assets/Uploads/343/343_persian11.pdf)
- Isbell reports Boyce: Jewish angelology "reflects to a large extent Zoroastrian belief in the yazatas" (second-hand; same JBQ link).
- Bremmer (via Ehrman): "little reason to derive Jewish ideas about resurrection from Persian sources", p. 59 of the 2002 book; oldest Avesta manuscript 1288 CE — [Ehrman blog](https://ehrmanblog.org/was-resurrection-a-zoroastrian-idea/)
- Ehrman's own dating argument (Persian rule ended 4th c. BCE; resurrection appears from the Maccabean period) — same page; a blog answer, authorship on the page not cleanly separated from reader comments.
- Smith 2001, first-hand but only his popular summary: monotheism explained by Assyrian/Babylonian empire and social change, not Persia — [Bible and Interpretation](https://bibleinterp.arizona.edu/articles/MSmith_BiblicalMonotheism)
- Smith, second-hand via Antia: doubts Zoroastrian dualism was the model for biblical monotheism but allows Persian religion "reinforced" monotheistic rhetoric; Zaehner (strong on individual afterlife, "not at all convincing" for eschatology); Russell (influence on apocalyptic "can hardly be denied") — [Antia 2012](https://AVESTA.ORG/antia/Views_of_Scholars_Refuting_Zoroastrian_Influence.pdf), [Antia 2020](https://AVESTA.ORG/antia/The_Zoroastrian_elements_in_Jewish_and_Christian_apocalyptic.pdf). Antia is a Zoroastrian priest and an advocate; his selections are not neutral.
- Dobroruka 2012 reports Gignoux's view that Persian apocalyptic is too late to be an influence, and Mesopotamian-origin alternatives — [Bulletin of SOAS](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0041977X12000043) (abstract, footnotes, references only)
- Boyce's chapter summary stresses the unsettled date of Zarathushtra and that Pahlavi literature was mostly written down 4th-10th c. CE — [Cambridge History of Judaism vol. 1, ch. 11](https://cambridge.org/core/product/identifier/CBO9781139055116A017/type/BOOK_PART) (summary page only)
- Agostini 2016 abstract: the question remains "a rich and exciting battlefield" — [Bibliographia Iranica](https://www.biblioiranica.info/iranian-and-jewish-apocalyptics/)

## Strongest proponent / sceptic (as asked)
- Proponent, first-hand: Shaked (typescript, undated beyond a 1984 folder, lay-audience, OCR-noisy). He still gives his own limit: monotheistic dualism, and (via Isbell) the chronology problem. Strongest proponent claims by Boyce and Zaehner are second-hand only.
- Sceptic, first-hand: Isbell (loanword absence, doctrinal mismatch, late sources). Strongest scholarly sceptics Bremmer and Gignoux are second-hand; Smith first-hand is context rather than a direct rebuttal of Persian influence.

## Sceptical and alternative pass
Searched for Barr, Bremmer, Grabbe, Smith, late-dating critiques and open review articles. Found sceptical positions (Isbell, Bremmer, Ehrman, Smith, Gignoux) and proponent positions (Shaked, Boyce, Zaehner, Russell). Nothing documented a route (no text or inscription showing a transfer); the live issues are chronology (Zoroastrian texts mostly Sasanian or later) and whether Jewish ideas had internal or Mesopotamian/Hellenistic sources. The page is therefore not one-sided, but it leans on second-hand reports for the main scholars.
