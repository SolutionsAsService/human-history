# ie-primary-papers: Primary aDNA and phylogenetic papers on Indo-European origins

## Ask
Open and quote the primary aDNA and phylogenetic papers on Indo-European origins (Haak 2015, Allentoft 2015, Damgaard 2018, Narasimhan 2019, Bouckaert 2012, Heggarty 2023, Lazaridis on the Anatolian question), quote each paper's own statement of what it shows and its limits, and the strongest published criticisms.

## Cited findings
- Haak et al. (bioRxiv v1 preprint, not the Nature text): Corded Ware Germans traced ~3/4 of ancestry to Yamnaya; "provide support for the theory of a steppe origin of at least some" European IE languages; "ancient DNA is silent on the question of the languages"; the PIE homeland "cannot be determined from the data reported here"; "not all Indo-European languages in Europe can plausibly derive from the first farmer migrations" — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/013433v1.full)
- Allentoft et al. (abstract only): findings "consistent with the hypothesized spread of Indo-European languages during the Early Bronze Age" — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature14507)
- Damgaard et al. (abstract only): steppe turned from Indo-European speakers of West Eurasian ancestry to Turkic speakers of East Asian ancestry (context, post-dispersal) — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0094-2)
- Narasimhan et al.: Steppe ancestry in South Asia matches Bronze Age eastern Europe, "likely" spreading Indo-Iranian; no ancient DNA yet for ANI/ASI — [PMC6822619](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6822619/)
- Bouckaert et al.: "decisive support for an Anatolian over a steppe origin" (103 languages, Bayesian phylogeography) — [PMC4112997](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4112997/)
- Heggarty et al. (abstract on Leiden repository page): root ~8120 yr B.P., not consistent with Steppe hypothesis; hybrid homeland south of the Caucasus — [Leiden](https://scholarlypublications.universiteitleiden.nl/handle/1887/3720269)
- Lazaridis et al. 2022: Anatolia had negligible Yamnaya impact; suggests West Asian Indo-Anatolian homeland; "Language is not encoded in the genome" — [PMC10064553](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10064553/)
- Lazaridis et al. 2016: Iran-related farmers spread north into the steppe (source of Yamnaya Near Eastern ancestry) — [PMC5003663](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5003663/)

## Sceptical and alternative pass
- Kassian and Starostin 2025 (open): cognacy coding in Heggarty 2023 does not separate cognacy from areal diffusion; tree and datings contradict historical evidence. Authors are Moscow-school and have a stake. — [HSSC](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-04986-7)
- Klejn 2017 with reply (abstract only): doubts that the genetic migration shows the IE language spread. — [EJA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/european-journal-of-archaeology/article/discussion-are-the-origins-of-indo-european-languages-explained-by-the-migration-of-the-yamnaya-culture-to-the-west/9E34F625389AF81B7EC16012F1D21FD9)
- Heyd 2017 (abstract only): centuries of connections, not a single Yamnaya-to-Corded Ware transmission. — [Antiquity](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/kossinnas-smile/8ABA3BD9132B7605E8871236065CD4E3)
- The two sides of the debate cut across each other: the 2012 phylogeny (Anatolian) and 2023 phylogeny (hybrid, ~8120 BP) both conflict with the Yamnaya-only reading, while the aDNA papers show migration but are silent on language by their own admission. Nothing found here shows language directly.
