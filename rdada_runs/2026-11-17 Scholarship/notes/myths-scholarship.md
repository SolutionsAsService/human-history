# myths-scholarship: Scholarship on Witzel's Laurasian mythology, Berezkin's motif maps and phylogenetic folktale studies

## Ask
1. What do independent specialists say about Witzel's Laurasian mythology and Berezkin's motif maps (reviews, critiques)? Open Lincoln 2015 if reachable, and any other critical review.
2. What do phylogenetic studies of folktales and myths find (da Silva and Tehrani 2016; d'Huy's Polyphemus and Cosmic Hunt papers) and how have critics responded?

## Cited findings
All quotes were confirmed with check_quote.py except where marked.
- Thompson (JFRR) calls the book "completely out of date and highly questionable scholarship" and the Laurasian/Gondwana scheme implausible and racist in framing — [IU ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/jfrr/article/download/38998/41327)
- Allen (JASO 2014): on his Thulung data the concept "merits further testing"; theory "may or may not survive"; sympathetic, by his own declaration — [Oxford anthropology PDF](https://test-anthro.web.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/anthro/documents/media/jaso6_1_2014_99_103.pdf)
- Witzel's own statement of the Laurasian assumption — [Harvard site](https://sites.harvard.edu/witzel/?p=385)
- da Silva and Tehrani 2016: ATU 330 "Smith and the Devil" reconstructed in PIE (Bayesian posterior 87%); Bayesian runs failed to support three of four other tentative PIE tales — [Europe PMC XML](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC4736946/fullTextXML)
- Tehrani 2013 (Little Red Riding Hood), the method paper — [Europe PMC XML](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC3827309/fullTextXML)
- d'Huy 2015, Polyphemus: 56 variants, 190 traits; text argues Palaeolithic currency (that sentence is in the PDF but check_quote could not match it, probably a line-break artefact; the candidate quotes the abstract instead) — [RMN Newsletter 9](https://www.helsinki.fi/assets/drupal/2022-12/rmn_09_2014-2015.pdf)
- Berezkin 2005 Cosmic Hunt: parallels "could be explained only by particular historical links" — [Folklore 31](https://www.folklore.ee/folklore/vol31/berezkin.pdf)
- Berezkin 2019: motif-distribution method and catalogue (55,000 abstracts, ~2,500 motifs) — [Folklore 75](https://folklore.ee/folklore/vol75/berezkin.pdf)
- Thuillard 2021 reuses Berezkin's database; motifs split in two main groups — [Folklore 84](https://www.folklore.ee/folklore/vol84/thuillard.pdf)

## Sceptical and alternative pass
- Witzel: Thompson (hostile) and Allen (qualified-sympathetic) both opened. Both note the discipline-wide objection that similarity may be diffusion/independent invention; Allen says Witzel dismisses universals but treats diffusion as secondary.
- Phylogenetic work: Urban 2025 (Nature HSSC, open access) is the strongest published sceptical paper found; it argues from a Quechua case that tree models fit poorly and calls the Bronze Age claim "astonishing". It does not reanalyse the Indo-European data. Scientific American letters (Hale, Richardson) raise missionary transmission and independent invention against d'Huy; d'Huy's reply appeals to Berezkin. These are letters only.
- No peer-reviewed critique of d'Huy's Cosmic Hunt or Polyphemus papers was opened. A phylogenetics blog (2014) and a LiveScience quote about the Pleiades paper surfaced in search but were not opened as sources.
- The brief is still lopsided on Berezkin's maps: no independent review of the catalogue was found; the sources on it are Berezkin's own, or Thuillard's, who reuses it.
